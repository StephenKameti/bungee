## 6. Production honesty

* **The hero is a three door tier.** B1 needs a sculpted resin crisp, rigging for the suspended halves and shards, and mains power for the spotlight; B2 is a moulded replica. Price them for the three supermarket hero doors only. B3 is the gondola end every supermarket buyer will actually accept: show it beside B1.
* **Dukas get no power and no floor.** B5 and B6 are steel and polycarbonate, unlit, hung or counter mounted, and built to take sun, dust and the long rains without fading or rusting: specify UV stable inks and powder coat.
* **The coal ring needs a socket.** B7 runs at supermarket and petrol station tills, which have power and generator backup. It is not for dukas.
* **Nothing floats without rigging.** The B1 shards and the B4 tumbling crisps sit on hidden pins; the B7 pouch sits on a clear rod. Add a rigging note to each fabrication brief.
* **Restocking decides the duka programme.** A clip strip with three pouches left looks abandoned. Three hundred dukas need a funded visit cycle and a clip strip the shopkeeper can refill from a carton in a minute; fewer dukas, visited often, beat wide placement that decays.
* **Category rules.** Crisps carry no special display or promotion rules in Tanzania that this run knows of beyond food labelling; confirm with the client's legal team, and keep children out of renders and duka units away from school gates if the client's own marketing code asks for it.
* **Price is the duka message.** The price plates on B5 and B6 are blank in every render; the client confirms the 30 g price and the designer sets it in post (placeholder TSh XXX). If the price moves, only the plate is reprinted.
