## 6. Production honesty

* **The hero is a three door tier.** A1 and A2 are custom fabrication with internal lighting and need mains power at the bay. Price them as the three flagship doors only, or procurement prices the hero against every door and kills the family. A3, A4 and A5 are the volume units.
* **Show the unit the buyer will accept.** Warsaw supermarket halls work to fixture specs and paid space. Put A4 (a gondola end in a standard footprint) beside A1 in the presentation.
* **Small specialist shops rarely have floor space or a spare socket.** A5 is a wall bay; A6 and A8 need no power; A7 runs on a hidden battery pack and needs a charge round on every merchandising visit.
* **Nothing floats without rigging.** The A3 pour is a cast resin ribbon on a copper arm, the bottle bonded to it; the A5 blossoms that drift free sit on hidden pins. Both need a rigging note in the fabrication brief.
* **The gift object is a cost line.** A8's amber block with a real linden sprig is cast resin, not glass, at gift volume. Cost it per unit before the tray goes in the deck.
* **Restocking is a budget line.** A3 and A4 show six bottles a step; a gap on a gondola end in the two weeks before Wigilia looks worse than no unit. Fund merchandising visits for the peak, or place fewer units.
* **Category rules.** Poland restricts the advertising and promotion of alcoholic drinks other than beer, with narrow exceptions for inside specialist alcohol shops and separated alcohol stands. Mead is covered. Before any unit is placed, the client's legal team must confirm which doors and which hall layouts qualify, and whether the price strips, the gift with purchase (A8) and any human figure in a published render are allowed. This could remove the supermarket units entirely.
