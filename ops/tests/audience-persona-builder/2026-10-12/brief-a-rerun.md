| Income / social tier | Statistics Korea household income quintiles, 1st (lowest) to 5th (highest). For people living alone, housing tenure is the marker everyone reads: an owned apartment, jeonse (a large lump sum deposit), wolse (monthly rent) in an officetel or a one room studio, a gosiwon (a tiny exam room). |

| Calendar | Seollal (Lunar New Year) around 6 February 2027, confirm the holiday dates. March brings new hires and new leases. Chuseok in September, confirm the live dates. Month end pay day on the 25th at many firms (estimate). |

### AUDIENCE ARCHITECTURE, Nuri Table / Launch, February 2027

---

### PRIMARY AUDIENCE (Tier 1): Seo Jiwon, 31, Sillim

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 27 to 36
Gender: Women lead (Jiwon); men living alone are a strong second
Income / social tier: The tier spans the 2nd to 4th quintile of people living
                      alone in rented studios and officetels. Jiwon sits in
                      the 3rd: wolse officetel, salaried. Weight media to
                      Gwanak, Dongjak, Guro and Mapo, not Gangnam
Location: Sillim and Bongcheon (Gwanak district); works at Guro Digital Complex
Household composition: Lives alone, 23 square metre officetel, 7th floor
Employment / income source: Payroll accountant at a logistics firm, salaried
Language dominance: Korean; the slang of living alone with friends
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (a Wednesday in late February)**
```
06:50, Wakes to the alarm in the officetel. Opens the fridge: a container of
her mother's braised lotus root from Seollal, two weeks old. Closes it.
Drinks a carton of soy milk standing at the counter.

07:40, Line 2 from Sillim station toward Guro Digital Complex. Reads a web
comic episode, then a short video of a creator cooking dinner in a studio
smaller than hers. Saves it. She will not make it.

12:10, Office canteen with two colleagues. The talk turns to what everyone
ate last night. Two of three say convenience store. Laughter, a small wince.

15:30, The team chat room on the messaging app. A colleague sends a coffee
coupon from the gift shop for her birthday next week. She notices how easy
sending food has become.

19:20, Sillim station exit. The convenience store by the exit has a lunchbox
offer at KRW 4,900. She buys one with a cup of instant soup.
BRAND MOMENT: the pickup locker beside the door. A chilled box that is
already hers would beat the lunchbox on the same walk home.

20:10, Eats at her small table while a long video plays on the laptop. The
plastic tray goes in the recycling bag, which is already full.

22:30, Her mother calls from Daegu. "Did you eat?" "I ate." She does not say
what.

23:15, Fills a basket on a dawn delivery app with vegetables for a proper
weekend cook, then empties it. Last time half went in the food waste bin.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To live alone well, not just alone
Primary tension: She wants to eat like someone looks after her, but cooking
                 for one wastes food, money and the evening
Self image: Capable and independent / wants to be seen as someone whose life
            is in order
Status signals: A tidy officetel on camera, home cooked dinners on her
                stories, a gatsaeng (productive life) routine
Fear / shame trigger: That her mother is right: she is not eating properly
Aspiration anchor: A small, ordered life where dinner is never the problem
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video on the commute and in bed; creators who cook for
               one, 22:00 to 00:00
Secondary media: The messaging app all day (chats, gift shop); web comics on
                 Line 2; platform walls at Sillim and Guro
Trusted voices: Her mother on food, colleagues on value, creators who cook
                for one on what is worth trying
Purchase channel: Convenience store at the station exit (four nights a week,
                  hypothesis to verify), dawn delivery app for weekend plans,
                  food delivery app on Fridays
Purchase trigger: A cheap first week, and proof it will not go to waste
Price sensitivity: Spends roughly KRW 25,000 to 35,000 a week on dinners
                   already (estimate); KRW 29,000 is the same money, not new
                   money
Brand loyalty pattern: Trial driven; loyal once a routine forms
Moments that matter: The two weeks after Seollal (around 6 February 2027,
                     confirm), when mum's containers run out; March, new job
                     or new lease; the 25th, pay day (estimate)
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it (new brand)
Current perception: "Meal box" means a commitment and a fridge full of guilt
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Fear of waste: one person cannot finish a box
What would change their mind: Portions sized for one, a pause button, and
                              seeing it fit the walk home
What they need to believe: This is enough for one, and none of it ends up in
                           the bin
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Cast her alone and content, never lonely. The
    visual world is the small table, the one bowl, the officetel light at 20:00.
What this means for MEDIA: Own the walk home: Sillim and Guro station walls,
    pickup lockers, short video between 22:00 and midnight, heaviest in the
    two weeks after Seollal.
What this means for COPY: Casual Korean in the words she uses for living
    alone. Lead with "enough for one", then the price per day.
What this means for ACTIVATION: Pickup lockers at Gwanak and Guro station
    exits, a free first week code printed on the locker door.
```

*"편의점 도시락 세 번이면 일주일 끝. 엄마 반찬은 냉동실에서 잠자고."*
(Three convenience store lunchboxes and the week is over. Mum's side dishes sleep in the freezer.)

**She is not lonely at dinner. She is tired of eating like nobody cares.**

```

Gender: Women, mothers of children living alone in Seoul
Income / social tier: The tier spans the 3rd to 5th quintile: parents who own
                      their apartment outside Seoul. Mihwa sits in the 3rd to

Location: Suseong district, Daegu (also Gwangju, Daejeon, Busan, the

Household composition: Lives with her husband; two adult children in Seoul
Employment / income source: Part time at a pharmacy counter; husband near
                            retirement
Language dominance: Korean, Gyeongsang dialect
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (the Sunday after Seollal)**
```
07:00, Kitchen. Portions the leftover Seollal food into containers for Jiwon:
glass noodles, pan fried fritters, braised beef. Labels each one in marker.

09:30, Church in Suseong. After the service the women talk about their
children in Seoul. One says her son eats instant noodles every night. Nods
all round.

11:30, The courier counter at the apartment complex. Pays for a chilled
parcel to Sillim. The box weighs 6 kilograms. Too much and not enough at the
same time.

14:00, The family chat room. Sends a photo of the parcel. Jiwon replies with
a heart and nothing else.

17:00, A long video about health after 50, then a short clip of a young woman
eating alone in a studio. Thinks of her daughter.

19:30, A friend shows her the messaging app's gift shop: you can send a meal
to someone's phone.
BRAND MOMENT: a gift that sends a week of home style food without a
6 kilogram box.

21:30, Calls Jiwon. "Did you eat?"
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To keep feeding her child from 240 km away
Primary tension: She wants to care for Jiwon, but the parcels arrive too big,
                 and she knows half goes uneaten
Self image: The family's cook and keeper / wants to be seen as a mother whose
            children are well
Status signals: Children with good jobs in Seoul, food that tastes like hers
Fear / shame trigger: That her daughter is thin, tired and too proud to say
Aspiration anchor: Knowing Jiwon eats well on a Wednesday, not just at Seollal
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: The messaging app (family chat, friends' group, gift shop),
               all day
Secondary media: Long video health and cooking channels; evening TV dramas
Trusted voices: Church friends, her sister, pharmacy colleagues
Purchase channel: Traditional market and the large discount mart for her own
                  kitchen; the gift shop for sending
Purchase trigger: A friend who already sends it; a gift that looks like care,
                  not a shortcut
Price sensitivity: One parcel's ingredients and courier cost her more than
                   KRW 29,000 (estimate); price is not the barrier, pride is
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal once trusted
Moments that matter: Seollal and Chuseok parcels (confirm 2027 dates); her
                     children's birthdays; the first cold week of autumn
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Bought food for her child feels like giving up
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Taste and pride: it must taste like home
What would change their mind: Hearing it is made the home way, and being the
                              one who sends it
What they need to believe: Sending Nuri Table is still her looking after
                           Jiwon, on the weekdays
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Nuri Table as mum's weekday kitchen, a gift she
    sends, never a replacement for her cooking.
What this means for MEDIA: The messaging app's gift shop as a media channel in
    the two weeks after Seollal and before Chuseok.
What this means for COPY: Warm, respectful Korean. "Send" and "for your
    child" lead; never imply her parcels are too much.
What this means for ACTIVATION: A gift card in the Seollal return parcel, so
    the next box she sends can be a Nuri Table week.
```

*"반찬 보내도 반은 버린다는 거, 나도 알아."*
(I know half the side dishes I send get thrown away.)

**Let mum send the weekdays. She already sends the holidays.**

### AMPLIFIER AUDIENCE (Tier 3): Kang Doyun, 27, Mangwon

**Role, reach, trust mechanism.** Doyun stands for the creators who cook for one on camera. His audience trusts him because his kitchen is as small as theirs and he shows the failures. When he says something is worth it for one person, it is permission to stop feeling guilty about not cooking.

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 24 to 34
Gender: Mixed; Doyun represents the group
Income / social tier: Income in the 3rd to 4th quintile, housing in the 2nd:
                      Doyun's half basement studio is famous on his channel.
                      Pay for reach and trust, not for status
Location: Mangwon (Mapo district); films at Mangwon market
Household composition: Lives alone
Employment / income source: Video editor by day; a cooking channel with
                            about 180,000 followers (illustrative)
Language dominance: Korean; fast, funny, mocks himself
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (a filming Saturday)**
```
10:00, Mangwon market for ingredients, filming the prices on a small camera.
12:00, Cooks one dish in the studio on a single induction ring.
15:00, Edits; the comments ask "how do you not waste food?"
18:00, Posts a 60 second short.
       BRAND MOMENT: a "one week, one fridge shelf" challenge with a box.
21:00, Live chat with viewers about food waste and electricity bills.
23:30, Answers messages from viewers who live alone and are tired.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To make living alone look like a choice worth showing
Primary tension: Paid partnerships could break the trust he built
Self image: Honest, scrappy / wants to be seen as the friend who has it
            figured out
Status signals: Comments, not followers
Fear / shame trigger: Being called out as an ad
Aspiration anchor: A book, a show, a small kitchen brand of his own
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video and long video platforms, his own channel
Secondary media: His fans' open chat rooms on the messaging app
Trusted voices: Other creators, his comment section
Purchase channel: Traditional market, dawn delivery
Purchase trigger: A product that makes good content and survives honesty
Price sensitivity: Counts every won on camera
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal to what his audience accepts
Moments that matter: After Seollal (the "fridge full of mum's food" videos);
                     the March moving season
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Meal boxes are for people who can afford not to care
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Fear of sounding like an ad
What would change their mind: Freedom to test it badly on camera
What they need to believe: The box survives his honesty
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Let creators test the box on camera with no
    script, failures included.
What this means for MEDIA: Seed 15 creators who cook for one in the two weeks
    after Seollal; boost the honest ones.
What this means for COPY: Their words, not ours. The brand only adds the price
    per day.
What this means for ACTIVATION: A Saturday pop up at Mangwon market with
    Doyun cooking from the box.
```

*"혼자 사는 게 문제가 아니라, 혼자 먹는 게 귀찮은 거지."*
(Living alone isn't the problem. Eating alone is just a hassle.)

**If Doyun can't break it on camera, it's real.**

### CROSS TIER STRATEGIC READ

Jiwon does not need saving; she needs dinner to stop being a daily negotiation she loses at the convenience store door. Her mother already solves this four times a year with a 6 kilogram parcel, which is the real competitor and the real ally. Nuri Table wins by becoming the weekday version of mum's box: sized for one, sent or bought, and vouched for by creators who live in rooms as small as Jiwon's. The anchor insight: **in Seoul, living alone is fine; eating like nobody cares is not.**
