## 4. Part 2: Written strategic brief input

### AUDIENCE ARCHITECTURE, Shema Smile / Back to School, January 2027

---

### PRIMARY AUDIENCE (Tier 1): Ineza, 15, Kimironko

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 13 to 17
Gender: Girls and boys; Ineza is cast as one of them
Income / social tier: The tier spans Ubudehe B to C households with a teen at
                      a public day school. Ineza sits in C: rented brick
                      house on a shared compound, walks to school, no phone
                      of her own. Weight media to radio and shared phones,
                      and to Remera, Kimironko, Kicukiro and Nyamirambo
Location: Kimironko (Gasabo); a public day school in Remera
Household composition: Mother, two younger brothers; father works in Musanze
                       and comes home at weekends
Employment / income source: Student, Senior 3
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda at home, English at school, a mix with friends
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (the first Monday of Term 2)**
```
05:45, Wakes before her brothers. Brushes fast at the outside tap with a
squeezed flat tube; there is almost none left.

06:30, Walks to school in Remera with two classmates. They talk about who
got a new phone over the holiday.

10:15, Break. A classmate offers a mint before English debate club. Ineza
wonders if that was a hint.

13:00, Lunch in the school yard: beans and rice, a small doughnut from the
gate seller. Sugar from the gate is the daily treat.

16:30, Walks home past Kimironko market. A kiosk by the moto stage has small
tubes at eye level.
BRAND MOMENT: a 25 g tube she can buy with her own coins.

18:30, Homework at the table while the radio plays a youth music show in
Kinyarwanda.

21:00, Borrows her mother's phone for short videos. A Nyamirambo skit about
a boy with bad breath at a party makes her laugh, then check her breath.

21:40, Goes to bed without brushing. The tube is empty, and the night brush
was never really a rule.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To be close to friends without worrying
Primary tension: She wants a fresh, confident smile, but the night brush feels
                 like a child's rule, and the tube is never hers
Self image: Clever, a bit shy / wants to be seen as confident in debate club
Status signals: A clean uniform, a phone, an English phrase dropped casually
Fear / shame trigger: Bad breath; being the one people lean away from
Aspiration anchor: University, a city job, a smile in a photo she likes
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video on her mother's phone, 21:00 to 22:00, comedy skits
Secondary media: Kinyarwanda youth radio in the evening; class chat groups
Trusted voices: Older cousin, best friend, the school health club patron,
                skit makers
Purchase channel: The kiosk by the moto stage; Kimironko market with her mother
Purchase trigger: Her own small tube, a flavour she likes, friends having it
Price sensitivity: Pocket money in coins; the small tube economy (RWF 400 to
                   600, estimate)
Brand loyalty pattern: Uses whatever the house buys; would switch for her own
Moments that matter: The first week of each term (January, April, September,
                     confirm 2027 dates); Kwibuka from 7 April, when the
                     brand goes quiet; the end of year holidays
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Toothpaste is a parent's purchase, not hers
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Not her decision; the night brush has no reason
What would change their mind: A tube that is hers, and a reason to brush at
                              night that is about tomorrow's smile
What they need to believe: Brushing at night is what confident people do
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Cast real Kigali teens in uniform, laughing up
    close. The world is the school yard and the walk home, never a bathroom.
What this means for MEDIA: Short video 21:00 to 22:00 on shared phones, youth
    radio after homework, the kiosk next to the moto stage, all in term week one.
What this means for COPY: Kinyarwanda with the English words she uses. Lead
    with closeness and confidence, not cavities.
What this means for ACTIVATION: A night brush challenge through school health
    clubs, with a free 25 g tube and a class chart.
```

*"Nta muntu ushaka kuvugana n'umuntu ufite umwuka mubi."*
(Nobody wants to talk to someone with bad breath. Native check before use.)

**Give her a tube that is hers, and a night brush that is about tomorrow.**

### SECONDARY AUDIENCE (Tier 2): Mukamana Claudine, 41, Kimironko

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 35 to 50
Gender: Mothers who run the household shopping
Income / social tier: The tier spans Ubudehe B to C. Claudine sits in C: she
                      rents, runs a stall, saves through an ikimina. Weight
                      radio and the market over digital
Location: Kimironko; a tailoring stall in Kimironko market
Household composition: Three children; husband working upcountry
Employment / income source: Tailor, daily and weekly income
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda; some French and English
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (the last Saturday of January, Umuganda)**
```
06:00, Porridge for the children; checks school shoes for Monday.

08:00, Umuganda: community work clearing the drain on her street. The
umudugudu meeting afterwards covers school fees and a health notice.

11:00, Opens her stall at Kimironko market. A neighbour orders a uniform skirt.

14:00, Ikimina meeting with six market women: contributions, school fees,
who found the best price on what.

16:00, Buys the week's food.
BRAND MOMENT: the toothpaste at the market stall, where she picks the largest
tube she trusts at the lowest price.

19:00, A radio talk show while she cooks; a dentist speaks about children's
teeth.

21:00, Ineza asks for the phone. Claudine reminds her to brush. Ineza says
the tube is finished.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: Children who are healthy and do well at school
Primary tension: She wants the best for their teeth, but the budget rewards
                 the tube that lasts longest
Self image: A careful provider / wants to be seen as a mother who manages well
Status signals: Children's clean uniforms, school fees paid on time
Fear / shame trigger: A child's toothache she cannot afford to treat
Aspiration anchor: Children who go further than she did
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Kinyarwanda radio, morning and evening talk shows
Secondary media: Family and ikimina groups on the messaging app
Trusted voices: Ikimina members, the community health worker, the pastor
Purchase channel: Kimironko market stalls, the butike near home
Purchase trigger: Price per week, and a health voice she trusts
Price sensitivity: Buys the 75 g tube (RWF 1,000 to 1,500, estimate) and
                   counts it against a week of food
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal to the tube she knows; switches on price
Moments that matter: School fees and supplies before each term (confirm 2027
                     dates); Umuganda Saturdays; Christmas and New Year
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: A new name is a risk with her children's teeth
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Trust and habit
What would change their mind: A health worker or teacher she trusts, and a
                              price that matches her usual tube
What they need to believe: It protects as well as the tube she knows, and her
                           daughter will actually use it
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Show her pride in a healthy child, never fear of
    the dentist.
What this means for MEDIA: Kinyarwanda morning and evening radio, with a health
    voice rather than a celebrity, in the two weeks before term.
What this means for COPY: Respectful Kinyarwanda. Protection and value, said
    plainly, with the 75 g price per week.
What this means for ACTIVATION: Saturday demos at Kimironko market, and talks
    by community health workers at ikimina and umudugudu meetings.
```

*"Umwana wanjye agomba kumwenyura nta soni."*
(My child must smile without shame. Native check before use.)

**Mum buys the trust. Ineza buys the habit.**

### AMPLIFIER AUDIENCE (Tier 3): Gisa, 19, Nyamirambo, and the school health clubs

**Role, reach, trust mechanism.** Gisa stands for the young skit makers whose videos Kigali teens pass around their class groups the next morning; the teacher patrons of school health clubs give the same idea permission inside school. Together they move the night brush from a parent's nag to something peers do.

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 18 to 25 (skit makers); teacher patrons 30 to 50
Gender: Mixed
Income / social tier: Spans Ubudehe C to D for the skit makers, B to C for the
                      teachers. Gisa sits in C. Pay in airtime, data and
                      equipment as much as fees
Location: Nyamirambo; films on the street and at friends' houses
Household composition: Lives with his aunt
Employment / income source: Skit maker; phone repair on weekdays
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda street register, English punchlines
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (a filming Friday)**
```
09:00, Opens the phone repair stall in Nyamirambo.
13:00, Writes a skit with two friends after Friday prayers.
16:00, Films on the street; neighbours gather to watch.
19:00, Posts.
       BRAND MOMENT: a skit series about the second before a close talk.
21:00, Comments roll in from students; he answers the funniest.
23:00, A health club patron messages to ask him to visit her school.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To be the funniest voice in the city
Primary tension: Brand money against street credibility
Self image: The neighbourhood joker / wants to be seen as a real creator
Status signals: Shares in class groups, being recognised at the moto stage
Fear / shame trigger: Being called a sellout
Aspiration anchor: TV, and a comedy stage in Kigali
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video
Secondary media: Guest slots on youth radio
Trusted voices: His crew, his comment section
Purchase channel: The butike in Nyamirambo
Purchase trigger: A brand that lets him be funny
Price sensitivity: Counts every franc
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal to whoever backs him early
Moments that matter: Term week one, when class groups are busiest; the end
                     of year holidays; silence through Kwibuka
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Toothpaste ads are for children
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Being scripted
What would change their mind: Creative freedom, and a joke the brand can take
What they need to believe: The brand gets the joke
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Comedy about the close talk, never about teeth;
    the brand appears only as the tube in a pocket.
What this means for MEDIA: Seed 10 skit makers in term week one and boost the
    two that travel furthest in class groups.
What this means for COPY: Their street Kinyarwanda, unedited, checked only for
    claims.
What this means for ACTIVATION: Gisa visits health clubs with the teacher
    patron to launch the night brush challenge.
```

*"Ubuzima ni ugusetsa, ariko ntusetse ufite umwuka mubi."*
(Life is for laughing, but don't laugh with bad breath. Native check before use.)

**Make the night brush a joke everyone is in on.**

---

### CROSS TIER STRATEGIC READ

Ineza does not need a lecture on cavities; she needs the night brush to belong to her, not to her mother. Claudine still buys the household tube on trust and price, so the 75 g tube must win her at the market while the 25 g tube wins Ineza at the kiosk. Gisa and the health clubs turn the habit from a nag into a shared joke. The anchor insight: **in Kigali, teens brush for tomorrow's closeness, not for the dentist.**
