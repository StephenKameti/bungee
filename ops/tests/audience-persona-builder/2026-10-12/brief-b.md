# Audience and Persona Builder test run, Brief B: Shema Smile, Kigali

Test record. Skill: `products/skills/audience-persona-builder/skill/` at the commit checked out on 12 October 2026, run exactly as SKILL.md instructs, as if a user had sent the brief below in one message. **Brief as sent:** Shema Smile is a fluoride toothpaste in 25 g and 75 g tubes, mass tier, launching in Kigali in January 2027 for the start of the school term. Objective: trial and a twice a day brushing habit among secondary school students aged 13 to 17. Channels: radio, social video, school health club activations, kiosks and markets. Define the audience.

**Where the run had to interpret the skill** (scored in SCORE.md):
* One message brief: the missing inputs (existing audience assumption, category context) carry working assumptions and the run proceeds.
* The East Africa module is loaded, but it is written for Kenya. For Rwanda it gives only the language order and says to treat Rwanda as its own module; there is no Rwanda tier system, area table, peer network or naming guidance. The run builds those from research, as SKILL.md says for a market without a module.
* Rwanda has no single published marketing tier scale. The national household classification everyone knows is Ubudehe (categories A to E, revised from 2020). The run uses it as the shared reference, labelled as a welfare classification, and adds area and housing signals. The skill gives no guidance for a market whose own tier system is not a marketing scale.
* Step 4's Youth / Teen variant sets "Tier 2 = the parent (the buyer), especially the mother". The run follows it.
* Kinyarwanda quotes are written by the run; the skill gives no rule for a native check or a gloss. The run adds both.
* No guidance on unverified figures; prices are marked as estimates.
* Part 2's Tier 3 "adapted profile" against the quality check's 6+ moments for each tier: Tier 3 gets all six components.
* Platforms, retailers and apps named by type.

## 4. Part 2: Written strategic brief input

### AUDIENCE ARCHITECTURE, Shema Smile / Back to School, January 2027

---

### PRIMARY AUDIENCE (Tier 1): Ineza, 15, Kimironko

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 13 to 17
Gender: All; Ineza leads because girls drive the brushing talk in class
Income / social tier: Ubudehe C household; rented brick house with a shared
                      compound
Location: Kimironko (Gasabo); day school in Remera
Household composition: Mother, two younger brothers; father works in Musanze
                       and comes home on weekends
Employment / income source: Student, Senior 3
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda at home, English at school, mixed with friends
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (the first Monday of term)**
```
05:45, Wakes before her brothers. Brushes fast at the outside tap with a
squeezed flat tube; there is almost none left.

06:30, Walks to school in Remera with two classmates. They talk about who
got a new phone over the holiday.

10:15, Break. A classmate offers a mint before the English debate club. Ineza
wonders if that was a hint.

13:00, Lunch in the school yard: beans, rice, a small mandazi from the gate
seller. Sugar from the gate is the daily treat.

16:30, Walks home past Kimironko market. A kiosk by the moto stage has a
bright display of small tubes at eye level.
BRAND MOMENT: a 25 g tube she can buy with her own coins.

18:30, Homework at the table while the radio plays a youth music show in
Kinyarwanda.

21:00, Borrows her mother's phone for short videos. A Nyamirambo skit about a
boy with bad breath at a party makes her laugh, then check her breath.

21:40, Goes to bed without brushing. The tube is empty and the night brush
was never really a rule.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: To be close to friends without worrying
Primary tension: She wants a fresh confident smile, but the night brush feels
                 like a child's rule, and the tube is never hers
Self-image: Clever, a bit shy / wants to be seen as confident in the debate club
Status signals: Clean uniform, a phone, an English phrase dropped casually
Fear / shame trigger: Bad breath; being the one people lean away from
Aspiration anchor: University, a city job, a smile on a photo she likes
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video on her mother's phone, 21:00 to 22:00, comedy skits
Secondary media: Kinyarwanda youth radio in the evening; class chat groups
Trusted voices: Older cousin, her best friend, the school health club patron,
                skit makers
Purchase channel: The kiosk by the moto stage; Kimironko market with her mother
Purchase trigger: Her own small tube, a flavour she likes, friends having it
Price sensitivity: Pocket money in coins; the small tube economy (RWF 400 to
                   600, estimate)
Brand loyalty pattern: Uses whatever the house buys; would switch for her own
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Toothpaste is a parent's purchase, not hers
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Not her decision; the night brush has no reason
What would change their mind: A tube that is hers, and a reason to brush at
                              night that is about tomorrow's smile
What they need to believe: Brushing at night is what confident people do
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Cast real Kigali teens in uniform, laughing up
    close. The world is the school yard and the walk home, never a bathroom.
What this means for MEDIA: Short video 21:00 to 22:00, youth radio after
    homework, the kiosk next to the moto stage.
What this means for COPY: Kinyarwanda with English words she uses. Lead with
    closeness and confidence, not cavities.
What this means for ACTIVATION: School health clubs: a night brush challenge
    with a free 25 g tube and a class chart.
```

*"Nta muntu ushaka kuvugana n'umuntu ufite umwuka mubi."*
(Nobody wants to talk to someone with bad breath. Native check before use.)

**Give her a tube that is hers, and a night brush that is about tomorrow.**

### SECONDARY AUDIENCE (Tier 2): Mukamana Claudine, 41, Kimironko

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 35 to 50
Gender: Women (mothers who run the shopping)
Income / social tier: Ubudehe C
Location: Kimironko; a tailoring stall at Kimironko market
Household composition: Three children, husband working upcountry
Employment / income source: Tailor, daily and weekly income
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda; some French and English
```

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (a Saturday, Umuganda week)**
```
06:00, Prepares porridge; checks school shoes for Monday.
08:00, Umuganda: community work clearing the drainage on her street. Afterwards
       the umudugudu meeting covers school fees and a health notice.
11:00, Opens her stall at Kimironko market; a neighbour orders a uniform skirt.
14:00, Ikimina meeting with six market women: contributions, school fees, who
       found the best price on what.
16:00, Buys the week's food. BRAND MOMENT: the toothpaste shelf at the market
       stall, where she picks the largest tube she trusts at the lowest price.
19:00, Radio talk show while she cooks; a dentist speaks about children's teeth.
21:00, Ineza asks for the phone. Claudine reminds her to brush. Ineza says the
       tube is finished.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: Children who are healthy and do well at school
Primary tension: She wants the best for their teeth, but the budget
                 rewards the tube that lasts longest
Self-image: A careful provider / wants to be seen as a mother who manages well
Status signals: Children's clean uniforms, school fees paid on time
Fear / shame trigger: A child's toothache she cannot afford to treat
Aspiration anchor: Children who go further than she did
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Kinyarwanda radio, morning and evening talk shows
Secondary media: Family and ikimina chat groups on the messaging app
Trusted voices: Ikimina members, the community health worker, the pastor
Purchase channel: Kimironko market stalls, the butike near home
Purchase trigger: Price per week, and a health voice she trusts
Price sensitivity: Buys the 75 g tube (RWF 1,000 to 1,500, estimate); counts
                   it against a week of food
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal to the tube she knows; switches on price
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: A new name is a risk with her children's teeth
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Trust and habit
What would change their mind: A health worker or teacher she trusts, and a
                              price that matches her usual tube
What they need to believe: It protects as well as the tube she knows, and her
                           daughter will actually use it
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Show her pride in a healthy child, never fear of
    a dentist.
What this means for MEDIA: Kinyarwanda morning and evening radio; a health
    voice, not a celebrity.
What this means for COPY: Respectful Kinyarwanda; protection and value said
    plainly.
What this means for ACTIVATION: Ikimina and umudugudu meetings with the
    community health worker; Kimironko market demos on Saturdays.
```

*"Umwana wanjye agomba kumwenyura nta soni."*
(My child must smile without shame. Native check before use.)

**Mum buys the trust. Ineza buys the habit.**

### AMPLIFIER AUDIENCE (Tier 3): Gisa, 19, Nyamirambo, and the school health clubs

**WHO THEY ARE**
```
Age range: 18 to 25 (skit makers); teacher patrons 30 to 50
Gender: Mixed
Income / social tier: Ubudehe C
Location: Nyamirambo; films on the street and at friends' houses
Household composition: Lives with his aunt
Employment / income source: Skit maker, phone repair on weekdays
Language dominance: Kinyarwanda street register, English punchlines
```

**Role, reach, trust mechanism:** Gisa's skits are what Kigali teens share in class groups the next morning. A teacher patron of the school health club gives the same idea permission inside school. Together they move the night brush from a parent's nag to something peers do.

**A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (a filming Friday)**
```
09:00, Phone repair stall in Nyamirambo.
13:00, Writes a skit with two friends after Friday prayers.
16:00, Films on the street; neighbours gather.
19:00, Posts. BRAND MOMENT: a skit series about the moment before a close talk.
21:00, Comments roll in from students; he answers the funniest.
23:00, A school health club patron messages to ask him to visit.
```

**PSYCHOGRAPHIC PROFILE**
```
Core motivation: Being the funniest voice in the city
Primary tension: Brand money against street credibility
Self-image: The neighbourhood joker / wants to be seen as a real creator
Status signals: Shares in class groups, being recognised at the moto stage
Fear / shame trigger: Being called a sellout
Aspiration anchor: TV and a Kigali comedy stage
```

**MEDIA & PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR**
```
Primary media: Short video
Secondary media: Youth radio guest slots
Trusted voices: His crew, his comment section
Purchase channel: Butike in Nyamirambo
Purchase trigger: A brand that lets him be funny
Price sensitivity: Counts every franc
Brand loyalty pattern: Loyal to who backs him early
```

**BRAND RELATIONSHIP**
```
Current awareness: Never heard of it
Current perception: Toothpaste ads are for kids
Barrier to trial/loyalty: Being scripted
What would change their mind: Creative freedom and a joke the brand can take
What they need to believe: The brand gets the joke
```

**STRATEGIC IMPLICATION FOR THE BRIEF**
```
What this means for CREATIVE: Comedy about the close talk, never about teeth.
What this means for MEDIA: Seed 10 skit makers in term week one.
What this means for COPY: Their street Kinyarwanda.
What this means for ACTIVATION: Gisa visits health clubs with the patron.
```

*"Ubuzima ni ugusetsa, ariko ntusetse ufite umwuka mubi."*
(Life is about laughing, but don't laugh with bad breath. Native check before use.)

**Make the night brush a joke everyone is in on.**

---

### CROSS-TIER STRATEGIC READ

Ineza does not need a lecture on cavities; she needs the night brush to belong to her, not to her mother. Claudine still buys the household tube on trust and price, so the 75 g tube must win her at the market while the 25 g tube wins Ineza at the kiosk. Gisa and the health clubs turn the habit from a nag into a shared joke. The anchor insight: **in Kigali, teens brush for tomorrow's closeness, not for the dentist.**
