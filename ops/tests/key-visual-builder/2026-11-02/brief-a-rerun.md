# Key Visual Builder rerun, Brief A: Halfshade Daily SPF 50+, Melbourne

Test record. Skill: `products/skills/key-visual-builder/skill/` as on branch `tests/key-visual-2026-11-02`. Run once on 2 November 2026, exactly as SKILL.md instructs, as if a buyer had sent the brief below in one message. Everything from section 1 down is the deliverable; the route book built from `assets/kv-library-template.html` is saved beside this file as `brief-a-rerun.html`.

**Brief as sent:** Hi. New launch for you. Halfshade Daily SPF 50+ is a face fluid in a 50 mL tube, light, with an invisible finish, made to wear alone or under make up. Market: Melbourne, launching 1 December 2026 and running through summer. Audience: city workers 25 to 40 who wear sunscreen at the beach but skip it on grey days and on the commute. The single message: Clouds are not cover. Channels: tram supersides, CBD street furniture 6 sheets, roadside billboards, social (feed, stories and 6 second bumpers), pharmacy shelf talkers. Mandatories: the pack on every KV, SPF 50+ visible, and the legal line Always read the label. Follow the directions for use. Budget is mid. No palette yet. Can you build the KV routes?

**What the skill decided:**

* No market module exists for Australia, so the market layer is built from the brief and from research into Melbourne's places, light, media and calendar (Market layer).
* The brief came as one message to run, so three questions are asked with working assumptions and the work proceeds (Step 1).
* The brief has no endline, so three are written once for the set and each route names its pair (Step 6).
* Six routes for a mid budget: two safe, three strong, one wild. The Budget row only speaks to low budgets; six was chosen to keep the motion work affordable.
* Bumpers are a moving channel, so the three fire routes each get a motion brief (Step 7).
* Every reprompt and variant is a full standalone prompt (Step 5, Step 7).
* Rerun after the fix list in SCORE.md: the legal line is now a Step 8 build sheet item, and the template now styles the gloss and the flag inside a copy line.

**Where the run had to interpret the skill** (scored in SCORE.md):

* None for the legal line: Step 8 now asks for it, with its zone held in every text zone and every motion brief.
* The Budget row covers low budgets only. Mid was read as six routes with the full kit.
* English is the working language and there is no second language in play. The local line is written in Melbourne's own street register, as Step 6 allows, and left to the headline where the headline already carries it.

### 06 Drizzle Burn (Wild)

**The idea.** A man in a hooded rain jacket stands in Footscray drizzle with a sunburnt nose, staring straight down the lens without a flicker.

**Why it wins.** A sunscreen ad that shows rain should not work, and that is why it does: the deadpan burn is the proposition proved on a face. (Hero portrait, High key clean)

**Set in.** Hopkins Street, Footscray.

**Layout logic.** One face, chest up, slightly left. The pack goes onto a clean plate lower right in post. Top 26 percent grey blur for the headline; bottom 9 percent for the endline and the legal line.

## 8. Close

**Fire immediately**

1. **Under the Grey.** The clearest argument for the proposition and the most ownable image; it works at every size.
2. **Sheer Cover.** The brand icon: a see through cloud can carry the campaign for years and is cheap to shoot.
3. **Violet Beads.** The safe pick that sells at the pharmacy shelf, with true evidence in frame.

**The disruptive alternate: Drizzle Burn.** Put it on the table beside Violet Beads. One is evidence, the other is a punchline with a face, and the room needs to feel both.

**What is missing**

* The real palette and logo files. The proposed palette is a working palette only.
* Which claims are cleared beyond SPF 50+ and the invisible finish; broad spectrum and water resistance are suspended.
* The full legal and regulatory wording for sun care advertising in Australia, checked by the client's regulatory lead.
* Tram superside sizes, door cut outs and the billboard site specs from the media owner.
* A UV figure with a source, if the client wants a number on route 03.
* Decision date and the shoot budget split between still and motion.

* Generated images carry no readable text and no logos. Wordmark, endline, pack art, legal go on in post.
* Pack label faces are generated as blank plates.
* Tool routing: Photoreal scenes and composites to the default photoreal image model; Under the Grey and Four Seasons as matched frames composited in an image editor; Sheer Cover can be shot for real with organza and a skylight; the bumpers by image to video from the approved stills with type, legal and logo code drawn on top.
* Casting: One cast figure in the set, described by place, weather and moment. The sunburn is make up.
* No hyphens and no em dashes in any client facing copy.
