The brief, read back

So everyone signs off on the same problem

Single minded proposition

One bar lasts the whole week's wash. (Verbatim.)

Product heroThe Siku Bright long laundry bar in its wrapper. Bar size not stated; the claim needs one. Assumed: one long bar (question 1).
Business problemSwitching. Mothers traded down to cheaper bars on shelf price. Win them back by moving value from price per bar to price per week.
Insight (proposed)She switched to save money. But the cheaper bar goes soft in the basin and is a sliver by Wednesday, so she quietly buys again before Saturday. She counts every shilling, so count the week, not the bar.
AudienceMothers 25 to 45 in Kenya and Uganda running the wash for a family of four to six, buying soap weekly at the market, the duka or the mini mart. In Kenya, in her words: kila shilingi ina kazi yake. (Every shilling has a job.)
ChannelsOut of home (roadside billboards), radio, social (feed, status and story, chat app forwards), open air market activations.
MandatoriesPack shot on every KV. Swahili and English copy. Price point visible.
PaletteProposed, none supplied: Siku Yellow #F5C400, Siku Indigo #1F2E7A, Line White #FAFAF5, Basin Blue #2F7FD1, Bar Cream #F2D46B. The real palette is listed under What is missing.
BudgetLow to mid, read as low: six routes, the minimum viable kit first, and routes that build from one plate or one cheap real shoot. Two colour print for the activation kit.
Price and currencyPrice must show. Kenya in KSh, Uganda in USh, one currency per execution. Not supplied, so marked placeholders: KSh XX and USh X,XXX (question 2).
Due dateNot stated. Decision date to confirm.
No gosNone stated. Applied: no identifiable competitor bar (the cheaper bar is a plain unbranded grey bar); no comparative claim without a test; no shaming the mother for switching; no poverty set dressing.
Market layer, two markets
  • Module. East Africa module loaded. Its Languages by country section sets the language order per country; Kenya is the fullest reference, and Uganda is placed from its own notes, never from Kenya's.
  • Two markets. Three routes are set in Kenya and three in Uganda, each in one named place. The three fire routes each carry a full variant prompt for the other market. Local line, price and endline are given per market.
  • Casting. By context: a woman with deep brown skin and short natural hair under a folded headscarf in a Kayole courtyard; a woman with dark brown skin and neat cornrows in a Nansana yard. Household casting is women led because the spine names mothers. No children in frame; their uniforms carry them.
  • Places. Kenya: Eastlands estates in Nairobi (Umoja, Kayole, Donholm) and Githurai on the northern edge, shared courtyard washing lines and painted shop fronts. Uganda: Ntinda, Nateete and Kireka in Kampala, Nansana on its edge, Mukono to the east; swept red earth yards, washing laid on grass to dry. No informal settlement as backdrop.
  • Light. January dry season in both. Nairobi: hard, high, crisp highland sun with short shadows. Kampala and Mukono: bright sun through a light equatorial haze, shadows a touch softer.
  • Language. Kenya: Swahili leads the copy, English second; everyday Swahili, no Sheng for a 25 to 45 household audience. Uganda: English leads business and advertising, Swahili is official but not the Kampala household language, Luganda leads Kampala and the central region. Uganda executions lead in English, carry the mandated Swahili second, and add a Luganda local line for a native writer to check.
  • Retail. Open air markets on market day, dukas and kiosks, neighbourhood mini marts. Long bars are often cut into pieces at the counter. Mobile money at the till; no payment moment is shown.
  • Media. Roadside billboards read at speed and in jams; radio carries reach outside the big towns, on national Swahili stations in Kenya and on English and Luganda stations in Uganda; chat app status and forwards outrun the feed; painted shop fronts are a real, cheap local medium.
  • Calendar. Pointer only, confirm the live year: the January back to school period, when uniforms are washed every evening and the wash load peaks; Easter; Eid; the festive season.
Open questions and the working assumption used
  • Which bar size carries the claim, and for what household? Assumed: One long bar, a family of five, about seven loads a week including school uniforms.
  • What is the shelf price in Kenya and in Uganda? Assumed: Marked placeholders: KSh XX and USh X,XXX, per day figures KSh X and USh XXX.
  • For Uganda, should the mandated Swahili stay on Kampala executions, or can Luganda take its place? Assumed: Uganda executions lead in English, keep the Swahili line as mandated, and add a Luganda local line for a native check.

Monday to Sunday

The same bar photographed seven times across one week, laid out like a calendar, and on Sunday there is still a piece left.

Why it wins. It shows the claim as evidence, day by day, readable in three seconds at a stall.

Product truth · Graphic flat · No people · Pack in scene · Umoja, Nairobi, Kenya
