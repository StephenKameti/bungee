# Key Visual Builder test run, Brief A: Alba single serve sachets, Lisbon and Porto

Test record. Skill: `products/skills/key-visual-builder/skill/` at commit d915ac5, run on 11 October 2026 exactly as SKILL.md instructs, as if a user had sent the brief below in one message. Everything from section 1 down is the deliverable as the skill specifies it; the HTML route book built from `assets/kv-library-template.html` is saved beside this file as `brief-a.html`.

**Brief as sent:** Alba is a mid priced ground coffee brand from Lisbon launching single serve coffee sachets in Lisbon and Porto. Objective: trial among urban workers aged 25 to 40 who skip breakfast at home. Single message: the first quiet cup before the city wakes is yours. Channels: out of home, social (including short vertical video), in store. Mandatories: the pack on every key visual; Portuguese headline with English secondary line. Budget: mid.

**Where the run had to interpret the skill** (scored in SCORE.md):
* The skill asks up to three questions; a one message brief cannot answer them, so each question carries a stated working assumption and the run proceeds.
* SKILL.md asks for three endlines per route when the brief has none. An endline is a campaign level commitment (copy-craft.md), so three are written once for the set and each route names the one it pairs with.
* Every Reprompt format gets a full standalone prompt. SKILL.md says "give the reprompt" but the template only has a short what changes cell, so a reprompt panel was added to the route book.
* The budget, which the brief states, has no row in the spine table; it is recorded as an extra row.
* Portugal has no market module, so the local layer was researched (first light and sunrise times, outdoor formats, coffee vocabulary by city).
* The headline language is Portuguese and the working language is Portuguese, so the local line field carries the Lisbon and Porto words for the counter espresso rather than a translation of the headline.
* Out of home sizes are the Portuguese standards (8x3 m roadside, bus shelter panels about 1185 x 1750 mm). Neither appears in the skill's adaptation kit.

## 1. Market layer

No market module on file for Portugal. Built from the brief and from research into Lisbon and Porto, as SKILL.md instructs.

| Area | What the market layer sets |
|---|---|
| Casting | Urban Lisbon and Porto are mixed cities. Cast by neighbourhood and moment, for example a woman with brown skin and dark curly hair on an enclosed glass balcony in Cedofeita. February work wear is layered: wool, knit, a work shirt underneath. No tourist styling. |
| Places | Lisbon: Arroios, Anjos, Penha de França, Graça, Campo de Ourique. Older buildings, tall sash windows, deep stone sills, lime washed or tiled facades, small kitchens. Porto: Bonfim, Cedofeita, Paranhos, Miragaia. Grey granite, tiled facades, enclosed glass balconies, steep streets. Postcard shorthand stays out: the yellow tram, the river bridges, the viewpoint terraces. |
| Light | February: first light about 7:00 and sunrise about 7:30 in Lisbon, a few minutes later in Porto. That half hour is the quiet window the proposition lives in. Lisbon light bounces off pale facades; Porto mornings often carry Atlantic mist over grey granite. |
| Language | European Portuguese, informal tu for 25 to 40, never Brazilian vocabulary: chávena, never xícara. The word for breakfast carries a spelling hyphen in Portuguese, so the copy is written around it. Local register: Lisbon's café espresso is a bica, Porto's is a cimbalino. English secondary line mandated on every KV. |
| Retail | Modern trade: hypermarkets, supermarkets and the small city centre proximity stores urban workers use on the way home. The coffee aisle is crowded with capsules and ground coffee; shelf talkers and gondola ends carry the launch, plus sampling. |
| Media | Street furniture panels at bus shelters and metro stations (about 1185 x 1750 mm, backlit, many now digital in both cities), 8x3 m roadside panels, short vertical video on the mainstream social platforms. |
| Calendar | Pointer only, confirm the live year: the clocks change on the last Sunday of March and of October, which moves the quiet window by an hour; the return to work in early January and in September. |

## 7. The routes

### 01 The Sill (Safe)

**The idea.** Your cup is already brewing on the window sill while the café across the street still has its shutter down.

**Why it wins.** The city's café has not even opened and yours already has, which is the proposition in one look. (Product truth, Warm documentary)

**Layout logic.** Cup and sachet sit on the sill in the lower right third; the empty street and the shuttered café fall away soft through the window; the upper 32 percent is the sunlit facade opposite, clean for the Portuguese headline with the English line under it. The pack lies forward on the sill so every crop keeps it.

**Motion brief**

* **Film line.** The street is asleep and the corner café is still shuttered, but one cup on one sill is already brewing; the viewer ends wanting that seven o'clock.
* **Spec.** 15 s, 9:16 master, 4:5 and 1:1 from the same timeline, 30 fps, 120 BPM (one beat 0.5 s, one bar 2 s, 7.5 bars including the final hold).
* **Build route.** Hybrid. Image to video from approved stills for each photographic movement (lamp, tear, pour, shutter), one movement per plate; type, pack lockup and logo code drawn on top so they land frame exact on the beat.
* **Assets.** The approved 9:16 master of The Sill; two extra plates from the same base prompt (the sachet tearing in macro, the café shutter across the street); Alba logo file; real pack shots of the sachet and the box of 10; brand fonts.

## 8. Close

**Fire immediately**

1. **The Sill.** The plainest statement of the proposition with the pack proud; cheapest to make and the strongest on the shelf talker and the bus shelter.
2. **Second Coffee.** The insight route. It reframes the café habit instead of fighting it, and it is the best short vertical film in the set.
3. **Long Shadow.** The most ownable image: only the sachet can throw that shadow. Built for the 8x3 m panel and for both cities.

**The disruptive alternate: Do Not Disturb.** Put it beside The Sill: the same morning told with wit. It also hands the launch a sampling device, a drip bag on a door hanger card left on apartment doors in both cities.

**What is missing**

* Pack format and real pack artwork. Every route assumes a drip bag in a palm sized sachet; a steeping bag changes every hero object.
* Brand palette and pack colourway. Both are proposed here.
* Launch month. Light and clock times are written for February; a summer launch moves first light to about 6:15 and every time in the prompts changes.
* Decision date and the media owners' specs for the 8x3 m panels and the shelter panels, including whether the shelters are digital (if so, the motion briefs apply to them too).
* Box size and price. A box of 10 is assumed in the calls to action.
* Claims. Only format facts are used (ground coffee, one sachet one cup, hot water only). Origin, blend, strength and brew time are not cleared and appear nowhere.
* A native European Portuguese copywriter to read every line aloud before it goes to the client.

## 9. Palette lock, standing rules, tool routing, casting

Proposed. No palette or pack artwork was supplied, so these five values are built from the brand name (alba, the dawn) and the two cities. They are replaced the day the real guidelines arrive.

|---|---|---|
| #1E2A44 | Alba Night | the blue hour, shadows, the sachet band |
| #F3C9A1 | First Light | the first warm light and the sachet body |
| #F4F0E8 | Lime White | lime washed walls, text grounds, label panel |
| #4A2F22 | Roast | the coffee itself |
| #7D8084 | Porto Granite | Porto executions only |

* Generated images carry no readable text and no logos; the type as terrain route goes to a type rendering image model or is composited in post. Wordmark, endline, pack artwork are placed in post.
* Pack label faces are generated as smooth blank panels; the designer drops the real artwork onto them at the build sheet coordinates.
* Tool routing: photoreal scenes, people and pack in scene on the default photoreal image model; readable type on a type rendering image model; any change to an approved master on the photoreal model in image to image mode, one variable per pass, with every brand mark cropped out of the reference first.
* Casting is described by context, the neighbourhood, the light and the moment, never by stacked category words.
* No hyphens and no em dashes in any client facing copy.
