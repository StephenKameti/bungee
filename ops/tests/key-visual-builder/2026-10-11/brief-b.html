The brief, read back

So everyone signs off on the same problem

Single minded proposition

One bar lasts the whole week's wash. (Verbatim.)

Product heroThe Siku Bright long laundry bar in its wrapper. Bar size not stated; the claim needs one. To confirm.
Business problemSwitching. Mothers traded down to cheaper bars on shelf price; win them back by moving value from price per bar to price per week.
Insight (proposed)She switched to save money. But the cheaper bar goes soft in the basin and is a sliver by Wednesday, so she quietly buys again before Saturday. She counts every shilling, so count the week, not the bar.
AudienceMothers 25 to 45 in Kenya and Uganda running the wash for a family of four to six, buying soap weekly at the market, the duka or the mini mart. In their words: kila shilingi ina kazi yake. (Every shilling has a job.)
ChannelsOut of home (roadside billboards), radio, social (feed, status and story, chat app forwards), open air market activations (stall A board, demo backdrop, bunting, aprons, canopy).
MandatoriesPack shot on every KV. Swahili and English copy. Price point visible.
PaletteProposed, see the palette lock: Siku Yellow #F5C400, Siku Indigo #1F2E7A, Line White #FAFAF5, Basin Blue #2F7FD1, Bar Cream #F2D46B.
BudgetLow to mid (stated; the spine has no budget row, recorded here). Routes favour one plate or one cheap real shoot, and two colour print for the activation kit.
No gosNone stated. Applied: no identifiable competitor bar (the cheaper bar is a plain unbranded grey bar); no comparative claim without a test; no shaming the mother for switching; no poverty set dressing.
Market layer

Loaded the East Africa market module supplied with the skill. Kenya is the fullest reference; Uganda diverges on language, see below.

  • Casting. By context: a woman with deep brown skin and short natural hair under a folded headscarf, photographed in a Kayole courtyard. Household casting is women led because the spine names mothers. No children in frame; their uniforms carry them.
  • Places. Kenya: Eastlands estates (Kayole, Umoja, Donholm) with shared courtyard washing lines and painted shop walls; Kisumu for a lakeside town. Uganda: Ntinda, Nansana and Mukono around Kampala, swept red earth yards, washing laid on grass to dry. No informal settlement as backdrop.
  • Light. January dry season: hard, high sun, short crisp shadows, which is what the graphic flat register is built on. Kampala carries a light equatorial haze.
  • Language. Swahili headline with English, as mandated. Everyday Kenyan Swahili; no Sheng for a 25 to 45 household audience. Uganda diverges: the module calls Uganda Swahili speaking, but in Kampala and the central region Luganda is the household language and English carries most advertising. Swahili stays as mandated; a Luganda line is proposed for native checking and flagged for a client decision.
  • Retail. Open air markets on market day, dukas, kiosks and neighbourhood mini marts. Long laundry bars are often cut into pieces at the counter. Mobile money at the till; no payment moment is shown.
  • Media. Billboards read at speed on arterial roads and at a standstill in jams; radio is the strongest reach outside the big towns; chat app status and forwards outrun the feed. Painted shop walls are a real, cheap local medium.
  • Calendar. Pointer only, confirm the live year: the January back to school period (uniforms washed every evening, the wash load peaks), Easter, Eid, the festive season.
Open questions and the working assumption used
  • Which bar size carries the claim, and for what household? Assumed: One long bar, a family of five, about seven loads including school uniforms.
  • What is the shelf price in Kenya and in Uganda? Assumed: Placeholder plates: KSh XX and USh X,XXX.
  • For Uganda, is Swahili the client's choice, or can Kampala executions carry Luganda? Assumed: Swahili and English as mandated, plus a proposed Luganda line flagged for a native check.

Monday to Sunday

The same bar photographed seven times across one week, laid out like a calendar, and on Sunday there is still a piece left.

Why it wins. It shows the claim as evidence, day by day, readable in three seconds at a stall.

Product truth · Graphic flat · No people · Pack in scene · Nairobi, Umoja

Master prompt

Photoreal model · 4:5
Vertical four by five overhead flat lay photograph, shot straight down onto a flat painted surface in solid Siku Yellow #F5C400 outside a shop in Umoja, Nairobi, under hard midday sun in the January dry season. Below the top 28 percent of the frame, eight objects sit in a precise grid of four across and two down, evenly spaced like the days on a calendar.
