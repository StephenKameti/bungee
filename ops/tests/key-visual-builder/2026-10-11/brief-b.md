# Key Visual Builder test run, Brief B: Siku Bright laundry bar, Kenya and Uganda

Test record. Skill: `products/skills/key-visual-builder/skill/` at commit d915ac5, run on 11 October 2026 exactly as SKILL.md instructs, as if a user had sent the brief below in one message. Everything from section 1 down is the deliverable as the skill specifies it; the HTML route book built from `assets/kv-library-template.html` is saved beside this file as `brief-b.html`.

**Brief as sent:** Siku Bright is a laundry bar soap sold in Kenya and Uganda. Objective: win back mothers aged 25 to 45 who have switched to cheaper bars. Single message: one bar lasts the whole week's wash. Channels: out of home, radio, social, open air market activations. Mandatories: pack shot, Swahili and English copy, price point visible. Budget: low to mid.

**Where the run had to interpret the skill** (scored in SCORE.md):
* The skill asks up to three questions; a one message brief cannot answer them, so each question carries a stated working assumption and the run proceeds.
* SKILL.md asks for three endlines per route when the brief has none. An endline is a campaign level commitment (copy-craft.md), so three are written once for the set and each route names the one it pairs with.
* Every Reprompt format gets a full standalone prompt. SKILL.md says "give the reprompt" but the template only has a short what changes cell, so a reprompt panel was added to the route book.
* The budget, which the brief states, has no row in the spine table; it is recorded as an extra row.
* Radio and open air market activations are in the brief but nowhere in the skill's adaptation kit or copy by channel table; the run improvised a radio script per route and an activation format set.
* Price is mandatory but the skill has no price instruction; a price plate with placeholders was added to every build sheet.
* The East Africa module calls Uganda Swahili speaking. For Kampala mothers that is wrong in practice; Swahili is kept as mandated and a Luganda line is proposed and flagged.
* No moving channel is listed, so no motion brief is written, as SKILL.md instructs.

## 7. The routes

### 01 Monday to Sunday (Safe)

**The idea.** The same bar photographed seven times across one week, laid out like a calendar, and on Sunday there is still a piece left.

**Why it wins.** It shows the claim as evidence, day by day, readable in three seconds at a stall. (Product truth, Graphic flat)

**Layout logic.** A grid of four across and two down sits on flat yellow: seven stages of the bar plus the wrapped pack in the eighth cell; day labels go under each bar in post; the top 28 percent is the headline; the bottom 14 percent carries the endline and the price plate.

### 07 Inside the Bar (Wild)

**The idea.** The bar is cut in half and, carved into the soap itself, is a tiny washing line hung with seven small garments: a whole week living inside one bar.

**Why it wins.** It shows the week is already inside the bar when you buy it; the one image here that stops a scroll, and a real soap carver can make it. (World build, Graphic flat)

**Layout logic.** The two halves sit centre frame with the carved cut face to camera; the wrapped pack lies behind; the top 30 percent is flat indigo for the headline; price lower right.

## 8. Close

**Fire immediately**

1. **Wash Day.** The human proof: a mother, a full line and a bar that is still a bar. Safest to approve and it runs on every channel in the brief.
2. **By Wednesday.** The win back argument for switchers, once the comparison is substantiated. It names the switch without naming anyone.
3. **Per Day.** Turns the mandatory price into the persuasion: divide by seven and the price argument flips.

**The disruptive alternate: Siku Saba.** Put it beside Wash Day: the claim built as a giant 7 of laundry. A seven reads in Swahili, English and Luganda, it can be shot for real with a drone in one day, and the same stunt can be laid out at the market entrance on activation day.

**What is missing**

* Bar size and the basis of the claim (household size and loads per week). Every proof line and the By Wednesday comparison are suspended until the client's wear test is in hand.
* Shelf price in KES and UGX, the recommended retail price legal line, and whether a fixed price can run on billboards for the whole flight.
* Uganda language: Swahili is mandated, but the household register in Kampala is Luganda. Client decision, and a native Luganda writer for the proposed line.
* Pack artwork, the brand palette and any emboss on the bar. All proposed here; the bar face is generated plain.
* Social format. Social is listed without video; if status video or short video is in scope, say so and motion briefs get written for the fire routes.
* Activation detail: which markets, stall and gazebo sizes, and the demo mechanic, so the backdrop and bunting can be sized.
* Radio: station languages (national Swahili, vernacular stations, Luganda in central Uganda) and spot lengths.
* Decision date.
