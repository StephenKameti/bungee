# Social Content Calendar Builder test run, Brief B: Ladha Njema cooking oil, Dar es Salaam, March 2027

Test record. Skill: `products/skills/social-content-calendar-builder/skill/` at commit 8268e03, run on 12 October 2026 exactly as SKILL.md instructs, as if a user had sent the brief below in one message. Everything from section 1 down is the deliverable as the skill specifies it; the interactive calendar is saved beside this file as `brief-b.html`. Ladha Njema is invented.

**Brief as sent:** Ladha Njema is a mid priced cooking oil made in Tanzania, sold in 1 L, 3 L and 5 L bottles and a 250 ml pouch. Build our March 2027 social calendar for Dar es Salaam. Platforms: photo feed, short video, and our messaging broadcast list of 2,300 shopkeepers and mama lishe (women who run street food stalls). Campaign theme: Ladha ya nyumbani (the taste of home). Audience: home cooks 25 to 45 across Dar, mostly women, from Kariakoo and Kinondoni to Temeke and Mbagala. Mass market. Mandatories: no health claims of any kind (cholesterol, heart, vitamins); Swahili first.

**Where the run had to interpret the skill** (scored in SCORE.md):
* The East Africa module was loaded. Its Languages by country section sets Swahili first for Tanzania, and the Wider East Africa note bars Kenyan hashtags and Sheng. Everything else in it is Kenyan: every fixed date, the hashtag sets and the content calendar. It has no Tanzanian public holidays and no Islamic calendar at all, so Ramadan, Eid el Fitr and the Tanzanian holiday list were researched. A run that trusted the module (which SKILL.md says takes precedence) would have missed the last ten days of Ramadan and Eid in a city where they shape the whole month.
* The global moments file names no moveable faith calendar either (no Ramadan, no Eid, no lunar new year, no Diwali), only "a major faith or harvest calendar date" at year end.
* Weeks: the month is split into four sections, with the last running 22 to 31 March, so the three day tail after Easter is not a week of its own for the one community post a week rule.
* Good Friday (26 March) falls on an anchor day. The skill says brands must not hard sell on sensitive days but also that every anchor day carries a post and every caption ends with a CTA. The run posts nothing on Good Friday and moves the Friday reach slot to Thursday 25 March.
* Pillar split: the FMCG row asks for 30, 35, 20, 15. Ramadan, Eid and Easter pull towards community, so the split runs 21, 29, 29, 21, inside both caps.
* Prices: the brief gives none and the skill has no rule for a missing price. The run uses marked placeholders, TSh [bei, thibitisha], and lists them under open items.
* Hashtags: Step 1 says 8 to 12 per post; Step 5 says 4 to 6 on short video and none on messaging. The platform rule was used.
* Caption length: Step 5 sets 150 to 300 words for photo feed posts; the Swahili captions are written to it.
* The interactive widget has no template or size rules in the skill, so `brief-b.html` was built from scratch with the same renderer as Brief A.

### Post 2 · Wednesday 3 March · P4 Education

**THEME:** Why kaimati soak up oil, and the three second test that fixes it.

**PLATFORM:** Short video platform.

**CAPTION** (short video platform, Swahili)
```text
Kaimati zako zinanywa mafuta? Tatizo si unga, ni joto.

Leo tunakuonyesha siri ndogo ambayo mama lishe wa Kariakoo wanaijua tangu zamani. Kabla ya kuweka kaimati, chukua tone moja dogo la unga na uliangushe kwenye mafuta. Jaribu leo kabla ya kufuturu, kisha tuambie matokeo kwenye maoni.

#LadhaNjema #Kaimati #Futari #MapishiYaKitanzania #Ramadhani
```
*Gloss:* Do your kaimati drink oil? The problem isn't the flour, it's the heat. Today, a small secret the mama lishe of Kariakoo have always known. Before the kaimati go in, drop one small blob of batter into the oil. If it sinks and sits, the oil isn't hot yet. If it rises fast, ringed with small bubbles, within three seconds, you're ready. Medium heat, not high: high heat burns the outside and leaves the inside raw. Don't crowd the pan; a few at a time so the oil keeps its heat. Turn them when golden, lift out, drain, then pour over cardamom sugar syrup. The result: light kaimati, a crust that crackles when you bite, and an inside soft as cotton. Try it today before futari and tell us how it went in the comments.

*Gloss:* If oil catches fire in the kitchen, water is your enemy. It's something we'd rather not think about, but every cook should know it. Pour water on burning oil and the water flashes to steam, throwing hot oil and the fire itself up and everywhere. Do this instead, three steps. First, turn off the stove or gas if you can do it without putting yourself at risk. Second, cover the pan with its lid or a large metal tray so the fire runs out of air. Don't lift the pan or run outside with it. Third, leave it covered until it is completely cool. If the fire spreads, get out, close the door behind you and call the fire service emergency number, 114. Prevention is better than cure: never leave a pan of oil unattended, especially with children nearby, and never fill a frying pan more than half full of oil.
