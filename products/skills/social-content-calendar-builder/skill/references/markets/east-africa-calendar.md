# Market Module: East Africa, Month-by-Month Calendar

Companion file to `references/markets/east-africa.md`, which holds the fixed annual moments list, weekly and monthly rhythms, sporting anchors, hashtag sets and language rules. This file gives the fuller month-by-month content angles, arcs and avoid lists. Read the relevant month section before building any calendar for this market.

The months below are written from Kenya and follow the solar calendar only. Ramadan and the two Eids move about 11 days earlier each year and can fall in any month: check the Islamic calendar section of `east-africa.md` first, and when Ramadan falls in the month, let it set the posting rhythm, above all on the coast and in Tanzania. For Tanzania, Uganda and Rwanda, take national dates from the fixed dates by country in `east-africa.md`, not from the Kenyan dates here.

---

## JANUARY
**National mood:** New beginnings, resolutions, school prep, a tight-money stretch after December spending.

**Key moments:**
- Jan 1, New Year's Day. Aspiration content. "This year" energy.
- Jan 2 to 10, Back to work/school. Routine restoration. Lunchbox content, back-to-school FMCG.
- Mid-Jan, the post-festive financial squeeze peaks, sometimes called "Njaanuary" locally. Brands that acknowledge it earn respect. Light, self-aware content.
- Last week, First payday of the year. Celebration content. "Imefikia" (it's arrived).

**Content angle:** Optimism tempered by reality. Brands that acknowledge "January is tough" while offering value win trust. Premium brands stay aspirational, January is when people set intentions.

**Avoid:** Excessive partying/indulgence content (the December hangover is real). Expensive product launches without strong value framing.

---

## FEBRUARY
**National mood:** Valentine's prep, school settling, short month energy.

**Key moments:**
- Feb 14, Valentine's Day. Runs 3 to 5 days of content. Couples AND families AND self-love (avoid alienating solo audience). Food/hospitality/gifting brands lead.
- Feb (varies), International rugby sevens season. National pride, sport, outdoor lifestyle.
- Mid-Feb, Schools' first half-term. Family content window.

**Content angle:** Love framed broadly, for family, for food, for Kenya, for self. Mass market brands use Swahili for Valentine's ("Mpendwa"-led content). Premium brands go editorial.

**Avoid:** Isolating content that assumes everyone has a romantic partner.

---

## MARCH
**National mood:** Long rains approaching, school half-term mid-month, International Women's Day energy.

**Key moments:**
- Mar 8, International Women's Day. Mandatory for any brand with women as primary audience. Must be substantive, not just a "we celebrate women" post. Actual stories, actual voices.
- The national world championship rally (varies, often March/April), speed, adventure, Kenyan pride, heritage.
- Mid-March, Schools half-term. Family and leisure content.

**Content angle:** Women, power, the long rains arriving (beauty/skincare brands, humidity content). Agricultural and FMCG brands connect to planting season in rural areas.

**Avoid:** Shallow IWD content. Kenyan audiences call this out immediately.

---

## APRIL
**National mood:** Long rains in full force, school mid-term, elections-adjacent content if applicable.

**Key moments:**
- Apr 1, April Fools Day. High engagement for brand pranks. Fake product launches, fake announcements with reveals. Works best for FMCG with strong social followings.
- Easter weekend (Good Friday to Easter Monday), 4-day holiday. Family, food, travel within Kenya. FMCG and hospitality brands, primary activation window. Travel, coast and Rift Valley destinations.
- Mid-April, First term exams for some schools.

**Content angle:** Rain creates two opposing needs, comfort and warmth (for mass market, home care) versus going out despite the rain (for restaurants, hospitality). Use weather as creative terrain.

**Easter content arc:**
- Thursday before Good Friday: Build-up / "long weekend sorted" content
- Good Friday: Quieter, reflective content. Many Kenyans attend church. Less commercial, more community.
- Saturday: Food, leisure, family activity content
- Easter Sunday: Family celebration, feast content
- Easter Monday: "Last day of the holiday" content, see-you-next-time

**Avoid:** Religious content that feels forced or commercial. Good Friday specifically, brands that ignore the day's significance and post hard-sell content get backlash.

---

## MAY
**National mood:** Long rains tapering, school term end approaching, Mother's Day.

**Key moments:**
- May 1, Labour Day / Workers Day. Worker appreciation content. "Behind the scenes" of your team. Community and human-first brands lead.
- May (2nd Sunday), Mother's Day. Highest-engagement Sunday of the year for family, FMCG, food, and personal care brands. Run content from Wednesday through Sunday. Full campaign, not just one post.
- Mid-May, School term ends. Children's content, school holiday planning.

**Content angle:** Gratitude and celebration. Mother's Day is a strategic window equal to Valentine's in impact for the right brands. Swahili content ("Mama"-led) consistently outperforms English for mass market.

**Mother's Day content arc:**
- Wednesday: Launch, "Something special is coming for Mama"
- Thursday: Product/gift content, what you're doing for Mother's Day
- Friday: Storytelling, "Mama's hands" / "What Mama taught me" type content
- Saturday: Last-chance / reminder content
- Sunday: The celebration post, emotional, visual, full

---

## JUNE
**National mood:** Short rains approaching, July school holiday anticipation, mid-year business review energy.

**Key moments:**
- Jun 1, Madaraka Day. Kenya self-governance anniversary. National pride content. Kenyan flag colours (black, red, green, white). Patriotic but modern, not government-style imagery.
- Mid-June, "Halfway through the year" reflection content works well for aspirational brands.
- End June, Salary + half-year bonus for some. Premium and treat content.

**Content angle:** National pride done with taste, Kenya as beautiful, Kenya as ambitious, Kenya as on the rise. Works for all brand categories. Avoid generic flag imagery, find the specific Kenyan thing that earns the patriotism.

---

## JULY
**National mood:** School long holiday begins. Families with children fully activated. July is leisure month.

**Key moments:**
- Full month, School holiday. Family content, children's content, holiday activity content. Maximum audience availability on weekends.
- Mid-July, Most brands' H1 results. Internal performance content for B2B/institutional.
- Last week, School holiday shopping begins (back-to-school prep early). Stationery, clothing, food brands.

**Content angle:** Play, family, fun. This is when FMCG brands can show product in leisure/holiday context. Hospitality brands push their strongest content. Children are visible in content (with appropriate safety standards, always with parents in frame).

---

## AUGUST
**National mood:** Long school holiday continues. Kenyans who can, travel. Nairobi estate culture, neighbourhood events, outdoor.

**Key moments:**
- Aug (varies), city restaurant week season. Hospitality and food brands' major window.
- Mid-August, Back-to-school prep intensifies. Uniforms, stationery, food prep content.
- Last week, School holiday ends. "Holiday ends" nostalgia content. New term anticipation.

**Content angle:** End-of-holiday content earns high emotional engagement. "Make the most of the last week" content for leisure brands. Back-to-school routine restoration for FMCG.

---

## SEPTEMBER
**National mood:** New school term. Routine restored. Rains returning. Work picking up.

**Key moments:**
- First week, New school term energy. "Fresh start" content.
- Mid-Sept, Sustainable agriculture and food awareness moments (global).
- Sept 21, International Day of Peace. Relevant for NGO and behaviour change brands.

**Content angle:** Routine, discipline, fresh starts. September is structurally similar to January but lower-energy. FMCG, laundry, household cleaning routines. Food brands, packed lunch content, school meal planning.

---

## OCTOBER
**National mood:** Short rains, Huduma Day, Mashujaa Day, global awareness months.

**Key moments:**
- Oct 1, International Coffee Day. Coffee brands, hospitality, and lifestyle brands activate. Kenyan coffee is a source of national pride, use it.
- Oct 10, World Mental Health Day. Handle with care. Authentic storytelling only, no brand washing.
- Oct 10, public holiday (named Mazingira Day from 2024; confirm the current name).
- Oct 20, Mashujaa Day (Heroes Day). National pride content. Stories of Kenyan heroes, community, innovation, resilience. More personal and story-driven than Madaraka Day.
- Oct 31, a global costume/novelty observance with low Kenyan cultural resonance for mass market, stronger for premium/youth brands with international audiences.

**Content angle:** Heroes and pride. Mashujaa Day is underutilised by brands, there is white space here for brands that tell genuine Kenyan stories.

---

## NOVEMBER
**National mood:** Short rains peak, year-end planning begins, a major shopping event approaching.

**Key moments:**
- Nov (last Friday), a major year-end discount shopping event. Sales-first brands activate heavily. Kenyan consumers have adopted it, especially for electronics, fashion, beauty.
- Mid-Nov, Christmas shopping starts for conscious planners.
- Nov (varies), Diwali. Relevant for Nairobi brands with an Indian-heritage diaspora audience (Parklands, Westlands).

**Content angle:** Value and deals. November is the month of "smart buying." Brands that offer genuine value without gimmicks win. The year-end discount event should be real, Kenyan audiences are sceptical of inflated prices marked down.

---

## DECEMBER
**National mood:** December energy is uniquely Kenyan, Christmas, Jamhuri Day, school holiday, year-end bonuses, travel to the rural home area (locally, "shags").

**Key moments:**
- Dec 12, Jamhuri Day (Independence Day). Biggest national holiday. Kenya flag content. Community events. "Kenya ni yetu" (Kenya is ours) energy.
- Dec 13 to 24, Pre-Christmas peak. Gifting, food, decoration content. Traffic content (Nairobi gridlock is real, use it with humour).
- Dec 25, Christmas Day. Warm, family, food. Minimal commercial content. Brands that wish genuinely outperform brands that sell.
- Dec 26 to 30, Post-Christmas lull. Rural-home-area content. Extended family. Rest.
- Dec 31, New Year's Eve. Countdown content. Year-in-review. "See you in [year]" content.

**Content angle:** December is the most emotional content month of the year. Nostalgia, gratitude, family, pride. The brands that earn this space have been consistent all year. December is not the time to introduce yourself, it's the time to celebrate what you've built.

**Rural-home-area content:** Travelling to the rural home area for the holidays (locally, "going to shags") is a deeply Kenyan December ritual. Brands that acknowledge it, the matatu journey, the grandmother's cooking, the estate going quiet, earn massive organic engagement.
