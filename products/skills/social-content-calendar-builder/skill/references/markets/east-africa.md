# Market Module: East Africa

Load this when the brief states Kenya, or another East African market, as the market for the content calendar. Treat Kenya as the fullest reference inside the region; where another East African market diverges from it, that is noted inline. When the brief names a different market entirely, do not reuse this file, build or load the matching one instead.

For the month-by-month content angles, arcs and avoid lists, see `references/markets/east-africa-calendar.md`.

## Language and code switching

Primary local language in Kenya: Swahili, alongside English. Other countries differ; see Languages by country. Code-switch register: Sheng, an urban blend of Swahili, English and street vernacular, dominant among youth audiences, especially in Nairobi.

- Formal register: English, for premium and B2B audiences
- Mass-market register: Swahili-inflected English or Swahili-led copy, for FMCG and broad reach
- Youth register: Sheng, for youth-first brands
- Local-language captions are cultural rewrites, not translations, the feeling must land, the words need not be literal
- Common Swahili openers that work: "Saa hii...", "Ukweli ni...", "Mama anajua...", "Wakati wa..."
- Never mix Swahili and English mid-sentence unless the brand is explicitly built on a Sheng or code-switch register

## Platforms that matter locally

A short video platform, a photo and stories feed platform, and a community feed platform (this one carries "neighbourhood group" energy, warmer, more conversational, more directly Swahili-friendly than the photo feed platform) are all in heavy daily use. A messaging broadcast channel is central to how small and mid-size brands transact directly with customers, order confirmations, reminders, direct replies. A professional network matters for B2B and institutional audiences. A microblogging and text platform carries an outsized share of real-time public conversation relative to its user base and is worth monitoring for reactive content.

## Posting rhythms

### Monthly social rhythms (Nairobi-anchored, broadly true across urban East Africa)
```
1st to 5th    End of month / beginning of month, salary has landed. Treat content.
           "End month" humour. Premium and aspirational content performs well.

8th to 12th   Mid-month lull. Engagement dips. Community and value content
           perform better than hard sell. Polls and questions.

Last week  Pre-end-month hustle. Value and discount content. "Get it before
           the month ends." Urgency works.
```

### Weekly social moments
```
MONDAY:    "New week energy", motivational. Ambitious. "Let's go."
TUESDAY:   Brand/product day, lower engagement than Mon/Fri, use for
           straightforward product posts
WEDNESDAY: Midweek check-in. Relatable fatigue humour. "Nguvu bado"
           (strength remains).
THURSDAY:  Anticipation. "Almost Friday." Lifestyle / treat content.
FRIDAY:    Highest organic reach. Weekend plans. Food. Fun. Community.
           Best day for engagement-driving posts.
SATURDAY:  Leisure and experience content. Not hard sell.
SUNDAY:    Quiet, warm, family. Rest content. "Good Sunday" vibes.
```

### Optimal posting times
6:00 AM (morning commute), 12:30 PM (lunch break), 5:30 PM (commute home / post-work scroll).

Confirm current year dates for any moveable observance (Easter included) before building a calendar around them.

## Currency and price points

Prices in Kenyan Shillings (KSh). Price sensitivity signals: sachet economy (single-use, low unit price) versus value-size versus premium, be specific about which applies to the brand and tier in question rather than defaulting to one.

## Sensitive moments

No promotional content on national days of mourning, disaster remembrance dates, or politically sensitive anniversaries. Check current-year public messaging guidance before scheduling anything near an election period, since campaign-adjacent content reads as tone-deaf and can draw real backlash.

## Wider East Africa notes

Uganda (Kampala), Tanzania (Dar es Salaam) and Rwanda (Kigali) share some rhythms with Kenya, salary-date effects, faith calendar, regional sport, but have their own independence and national days, their own language mixes (Luganda and English code switching in Uganda; Swahili as the first language of business and media in Tanzania; Kinyarwanda alongside French and English in Rwanda), and their own hashtag conventions. Do not apply Kenyan hashtags or Sheng to these markets. Regional food and hospitality hashtags worth using where the brief covers that market specifically: #KampalaEats (Uganda), #DarEats (Tanzania). Treat each of these as its own module when the work is specific to it, rather than folding it silently into the Kenya reference.

## Languages by country

Set the language order from the country, not from Kenya.

- Kenya: English and Swahili are both official. Business and advertising run in English; the local line is Swahili, with Sheng for young urban audiences in Nairobi.
- Uganda: English leads in business and advertising. Swahili is official but is not the household language in Kampala. Luganda leads in Kampala and the central region, and other regions use their own languages (for example Runyankore in the west, Acholi or Lango in the north, Lusoga in the east). Write the local line in Luganda for Kampala and have a native writer check it.
- Tanzania: Swahili leads in daily life, media, business and advertising. Write Swahili first and English second, if at all. Coastal and Zanzibar Swahili differ from upcountry speech.
- Rwanda: Kinyarwanda leads, with English and French in business.
- Zambia: English leads in business. Bemba, Nyanja and other languages lead by region, not Swahili.
- Never reuse a Kenyan Sheng line unchanged in another country, and when a brief spans two countries write a local line for each.
