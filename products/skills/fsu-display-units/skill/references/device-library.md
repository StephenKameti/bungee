# The Theatrical Device Library

One device per unit. Executed completely. Never repeated within a family. This file expands each device family with usage notes, size suitability, cost tier, and the prompt language that makes each one render well.

## 1. Oversized pack as architecture

The unit IS the pack at 1.5 to 2.5 m. Not a poster of the pack, a dimensional, exactly faithful replica that functions as the display structure, usually with a shelf ring, wing shelves, or a recessed bay holding real product at reach height.

- **Best at:** floor units, window installs, launch moments. Countertop versions read as toys; avoid below ~1.2 m.
- **Cost tier:** highest. Sculpted, hardcoated, painted; internal lighting common.
- **Prompt language that works:** "a two metre tall sculptural replica of the [pack], finished in smooth matte lacquer reproducing the real label exactly at scale"; give the replica a job ("appears to be filled: the lower two thirds glow softly from within"); anchor it on "a circular pale stone base" or plinth so it reads fabricated, not pasted.
- **Watch for:** the pack fidelity block is critical here. The generator must copy the attached pack, not invent one. Shoot from a low angle for monumental presence.

## 2. Product in frozen motion

A single suspended moment of use: the drop mid fall, the pour mid stream, the crumble mid air, lather mid rise. Rendered glossy and lit as the jewel of the unit, held on slim brass arms or hidden mounts.

- **Best at:** medium units and counter glorifiers. The moment must be readable in half a second from ten metres.
- **Cost tier:** medium to high (resin/acrylic sculpt plus mount).
- **Prompt language:** "a single giant suspended drop, perfectly formed, glossy and translucent, catching the light, frozen mid fall"; give the motion a destination ("falls toward a shallow stone bowl where soft concentric ripple rings are carved as if the surface had just been touched").
- **Watch for:** one moment only. A drop AND a splash AND a pour is noise. In the honesty flags, note the suspended element needs rigging.

## 3. Campaign device in 3D

Translate the campaign's key visual mechanic into an object: the campaign mirror becomes a real mirror arch with shelf wings; the campaign doorway becomes a walk up portal; a mascot becomes a sculpted greeter. This is the device that makes a unit ownable, competitors can copy a glorifier, they cannot copy your campaign.

- **Best at:** any size; scales down well (a full length mirror arch becomes a counter vanity mirror).
- **Cost tier:** varies with the device.
- **Prompt language:** build the interaction in: "a person looking into the mirror sees themselves framed by the full range"; "the shopper becomes the key visual while standing at the shelf."
- **Watch for:** overuse across the wider campaign. If the device already appears at six other touchpoints, choose a different family for retail or the campaign has one idea instead of a platform. Say so.

## 4. Light as material

Internal glow, a halo ring, a lit horizon line, a product floating in a circle of light. Light is the device; the structure is quiet.

- **Best at:** premium registers; environments that can go dim around the unit (beauty stores, duty free). Weak in bright supermarket light, flag it.
- **Cost tier:** medium; needs mains power at the bay (always flag).
- **Prompt language:** "a huge perfect circle cut through the monolith, its inner rim glowing with warm amber light like a low sun, the glow washing down the face of the unit"; "concealed warm lighting with no visible LED strips or fittings", that clause is mandatory, visible strips are the single fastest way to lose premium; close the scene with "the unit is the brightest object in frame, the store falling softly dark behind."
- **Watch for:** one light idea per unit. Halo OR internal glow OR lit horizon.

## 5. Narrative structure

The form of the unit draws the product story in space. A regimen as ascending platforms. A continuous lacquered ribbon folding into shelves, one per step of the ritual. A left to right journey from raw ingredient to finished product. The unit teaches by standing there.

- **Best at:** ranges with a genuine sequence (skincare routines, cooking steps, before/during/after). Do not force a narrative onto a range that has none.
- **Cost tier:** medium.
- **Prompt language:** name the sequence physically: "a single continuous ribbon of smooth matte lacquered steel rises in one flowing S curve, folding to form three platforms at ascending heights"; assign each platform its SKU by name; "each platform washed by a concealed downlight so the groups glow in sequence."
- **Watch for:** the sequence must survive restocking, three platforms, not eleven.

## 6. Glorifier elevation

One hero SKU raised on a column, under glass, or in a lit niche, treated as an artifact; the supporting range in fitted recesses around the base like instruments in a case. Borrowed from luxury and watches; lands hard in pharmacy and mass retail precisely because nothing else there does it.

- **Best at:** counter tops and small footprints; also the top of a floor unit.
- **Cost tier:** lowest of the high impact devices. This is the default recommendation when budget is tight but the client wants premium.
- **Prompt language:** "a single [hero SKU] elevated on a slim brushed brass column, enclosed under a clear glass dome, lit from below through the base so the bottle glows"; "a shallow ring tray holds the remaining products lying in fitted recesses."
- **Watch for:** the hero must be undisputed. Five products under five domes is a vitrine, not a glorifier.

## 7. Sculpted texture wall

The product's sensory signature rendered as dimensional relief: matte spheres of lather emerging from a back wall, a grain texture carved across a panel, foam, petals, crumb. Gives a flat back panel a reason to exist and photographs beautifully at 45 degrees.

- **Best at:** medium units; as the supporting surface behind an oversized pack or motion device it is the one permitted pairing, texture wall is treated as surface, not as a second device.
- **Cost tier:** medium (CNC or cast relief).
- **Prompt language:** "sculpted relief forms of soft lather bubbles, matte ivory spheres of varying sizes emerging from the wall in three dimensions, a few appearing to drift free on hidden mounts."
- **Watch for:** keep relief monochrome in a brand neutral; coloured texture reads as themed party, not premium.

## Choosing across a family

A strong 7 to 9 unit family typically spends its devices like this: architecture or light on the floor hero, motion or narrative on the mediums, glorifier and a miniature of the campaign device on the counters, texture as a supporting surface somewhere. The client should be able to lay all renders side by side and see seven different ideas wearing one uniform.
