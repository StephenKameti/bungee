# Market Module: East Africa

Load this when the brief states Kenya, or another East African market, as the market for the display units. Treat Kenya as the fullest reference inside the region.

---

## Casting notes

Cast by context, never by stacked category. Do not write "Black Kenyan people only." Write the setting and let it carry the specificity: "shoppers with deep brown skin, natural hair and braids common, in a Nairobi supermarket aisle." A human figure included for scale should be doing something believable in that retail setting, reaching for a shelf, reading a carton, glancing at the unit in passing, never posing to camera.

## Places and settings

- Pharmacy and beauty retail in Kenya is a distinct, usually smaller footprint format than a full supermarket, counters are common, floor space for a full FSU is limited and often negotiated
- Large modern chain supermarkets exist mainly in urban malls and higher income neighbourhoods; describe generically ("a large modern supermarket chain") rather than naming a retailer
- Open air markets, kiosks and small independent shops are a major channel outside major cities and for lower cost categories; a display unit brief for this channel needs a much smaller, more rugged format, not a scaled down version of the mall unit
- Outside Nairobi, Mombasa's coastal retail carries warmer, more humid feeling light and a different architectural texture; do not reuse Nairobi's retail texture unchanged

## Light and season

Indoor retail lighting is the dominant condition for most FSU renders (fluorescent or LED strip lighting, often cooler and flatter than the unit's own concealed lighting), but where a scene shows daylight through a shopfront or mall atrium, follow the seasonal pattern: hot dry dusty light January to March, softer overcast light during the long rains April to June, clearer high altitude light July to September in Nairobi specifically (Mombasa stays hazier and more humid looking year round), and a mix of bright and sudden rain October to December.

## Language and local line rules, with examples

Any wordmark or shelf talker text stays blank for the designer, per the standing text lockdown rule, so a local line rarely renders inside the image itself. Where a brief calls for it anyway (a mascot's spoken line represented as a small sign, for example), follow the same rule as the key visual work: English is the working language, Swahili and Sheng are the local register, blended naturally rather than translated, and tested against the specific audience named in the brief. See the key visual builder skill's market module for worked example lines.

## Retail environment

- Chains negotiate fixture specs and paid listings; the family should include a unit the buyer will actually accept (medium and counter top) alongside the ambition unit (the floor hero)
- Mains power at the bay is not guaranteed at every door; flag it whenever a device needs it
- Restocking visits are less frequent at smaller, more remote outlets; a unit that depends on tidy product facing will look worse for longer between visits, factor that into the honesty flags

## Media landscape

Not directly applicable to display units beyond the retail environment itself. A unit occasionally gets photographed and shared informally by staff or shoppers (a device with strong visual theatre is more likely to travel this way); it is worth designing for that as a bonus, not a requirement.

## Cultural moments, pointer only

Festive season (late November through December) and back to school periods (January, May) are the two windows most likely to justify a seasonal display unit or a limited time theatrical device. Confirm current year dates before building a calendar around them; several relevant observances (Ramadan, Eid, Easter) move year to year.
