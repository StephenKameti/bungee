# Market Module: East Africa

Load this when the brief states Kenya, or another East African market, as the market for the audience work. Treat Kenya, and Nairobi specifically, as the fullest reference inside the region. For Uganda, Tanzania or Rwanda, use the pointers under Wider East Africa and build the rest from research; never reuse the Kenya tables there. When the brief names a market outside the region, do not use this file, build or load the matching one instead.

## Language and code switching

Primary local language in Kenya: Swahili, alongside English. Other countries differ; see Languages by country. Code switch register: Sheng, an urban blend of Swahili, English and street vernacular.

- Swahili primary for mass market
- English and Swahili mix for the upper income tiers (LSM A to B)
- Sheng layer for youth audiences

## Income / social tier system

Kenya uses LSM (Living Standards Measure) bands, A being highest income and E being survival economy:

- **LSM A**: Karen, Runda, Muthaiga, Gigiri, high income, international exposure
- **LSM B**: Westlands, Kilimani, Lavington, Kileleshwa, upper middle, aspirational
- **LSM C**: South B/C, Langata, Embakasi, Roysambu, middle income, value conscious
- **LSM D**: Kayole, Dandora, Githurai, Kawangware, lower middle, driven by the daily hustle
- **LSM E**: Mathare, Mukuru, Kibera, survival economy, reliant on community

## Key cultural touchpoints

- **Chama**: Women's savings and investment groups, primary peer influence network for LSM C to D women
- **Estate group chats**: Neighbourhood alerts, recommendations, group buying, carried on a messaging broadcast platform
- **School gate**: 7am and 3:30pm, the main peer to peer brand conversation moment for mums
- **Informal transit culture**: Youth culture specific to each route, music, street media, centred on the shared minibus system
- **Corner kiosk (duka ya kona)**: Neighbourhood kiosk, first and last purchase point for LSM C to E
- **Church**: Sunday social infrastructure for LSM B to D families
- **Football ground**: Saturday and Sunday male social hub for LSM C to E

## Local purchase channels

Corner kiosk and small independent shops (LSM C to E, first and last purchase point), neighbourhood and roadside markets, local supermarket chains of middle size (LSM B to C), large format malls and hypermarkets (LSM A to B), group buying through estate chats paid by mobile money, online delivery (growing, concentrated in LSM A to B).

## Media landscape

Radio is the widest reach medium, and much of it runs in local languages beyond Swahili and English, strongest outside Nairobi. Evening TV news for families. The dominant messaging app's status updates and groups move faster than any feed. Short video for under 30s, often watched on shared data bundles. Billboards on the arterial roads are read in traffic; the shared minibuses carry their own street media.

## Cultural calendar

Confirm the live year; school term dates and the Eid dates move.
- January: back to school, uniforms and fees, the long squeeze after the festive season
- Easter, and Eid al Fitr and Eid al Adha, which move each year
- School terms open roughly in January, late April and late August
- Madaraka Day 1 June, Mashujaa Day 20 October, Jamhuri Day 12 December
- December: Nairobi empties upcountry for Christmas; spend peaks before, budgets tighten after
- Month end: pay day lifts spend in the salaried tiers

## Currency and price signals

Prices in Kenyan Shillings (KSh). Small format, single use packaging (the "sachet economy") signals value consciousness and dominates LSM C to E; value size signals household planning in the middle tier; premium and full size signals LSM A to B.

## Naming conventions

Match the name to the estate and tier. Worked examples: Zawadi for Lavington (LSM A to B), Atieno for Kayole (LSM D), Wanjiru for South B (LSM B to C).

## Wider East Africa

Uganda (Kampala), Tanzania (Dar es Salaam) and Rwanda (Kigali) each have their own neighbourhood geography, tier terminology and peer networks. Do not reuse the Kenya LSM estate table for these markets. Start from the pointers below, research the rest, and confirm the tier cut with the client's research partner.

- **Uganda.** Tier reference: research houses commonly report by socioeconomic class (SEC A to E). Areas to start from: Kololo, Nakasero, Muyenga (upper); Ntinda, Bukoto, Naalya (middle); Kawempe, Nansana, Kisenyi (lower). Peer networks: village savings and loan groups, SACCOs, church, the boda boda stage. Calendar: Martyrs Day, 3 June. Currency: Ugandan Shillings (USh).
- **Tanzania.** Tier reference: socioeconomic class (SEC A to E) in most research. Areas to start from: Masaki, Oyster Bay (upper); Mikocheni, Mbezi Beach, Sinza (middle); Temeke, Mbagala, Manzese, Tandale (lower); Kariakoo as the trading heart. Peer networks: VICOBA savings groups, upatu rotating savings, kitchen parties, mama lishe food vendors, the genge vegetable stall, daladala routes. Calendar: Saba Saba (7 July), Nane Nane (8 August), Ramadan, strongly observed on the coast and in Zanzibar. Currency: Tanzanian Shillings (TSh).
- **Rwanda.** Tier reference: Ubudehe, the national household classification (A highest to E, households unable to work), revised from 2020. It is a welfare tool, not a research scale, so add visible markers (housing, school type, transport) and confirm the research partner's own cut. Areas to start from: Nyarutarama, Kiyovu, Kimihurura (upper); Kacyiru, Kibagabaga, Remera, Kicukiro (middle); Nyamirambo, Gatsata, Kimisagara, Nyabugogo (mixed and lower). Peer networks: ibimina savings groups, umudugudu neighbourhood meetings, Umuganda community work on the last Saturday of the month, the moto taxi stage, the butike corner shop, Kimironko and Nyabugogo markets. Calendar: Kwibuka from 7 April, when brands go quiet; Kwibohora, 4 July; Umuganura, early August. Currency: Rwandan Francs (RWF).

## Languages by country

Set the language order from the country, not from Kenya.

- Kenya: English and Swahili are both official. Business and advertising run in English; the local line is Swahili, with Sheng for young urban audiences in Nairobi.
- Uganda: English leads in business and advertising. Swahili is official but is not the household language in Kampala. Luganda leads in Kampala and the central region, and other regions use their own languages (for example Runyankore in the west, Acholi or Lango in the north, Lusoga in the east). Write the local line in Luganda for Kampala and have a native writer check it.
- Tanzania: Swahili leads in daily life, media, business and advertising. Write Swahili first and English second, if at all. Coastal and Zanzibar Swahili differ from upcountry speech.
- Rwanda: Kinyarwanda leads, with English and French in business. Young people in Kigali mix English words into Kinyarwanda.
- Zambia: English leads in business. Bemba, Nyanja and other languages lead by region, not Swahili.
- Never reuse a Kenyan Sheng line unchanged in another country, and when a brief spans two countries write a local line for each.
