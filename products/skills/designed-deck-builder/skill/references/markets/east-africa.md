# Market Module: East Africa

This is a worked market module. Load it when the brief states Kenya, or another East African market, as the brand's home market or the audience's market. Treat Kenya as the fullest reference inside the region; where another East African market diverges from it, that is noted inline. When the brief names a different market entirely, do not reuse this file, build or load the matching one instead.

---

## Audience and society

Nairobi neighbourhoods carry distinct class and character signals worth designing to: Kilimani and Lavington read upper middle class and leafy; Eastlands neighbourhoods like Umoja or Buruburu read working and lower middle class, dense low rise housing, shared minibus culture visible; informal settlements should only inform a deck when the brief is explicitly about that context, handled with care and never as generic set dressing. Chamas, informal savings and investment groups, are a genuine social and economic structure worth reflecting as texture in decks about community, saving or group buying. Household and shopper decisions are often women led in FMCG categories, but confirm against the brief rather than assume; trade and distributor audiences skew male in most on the ground FMCG contexts.

## Language and copy rules

In Kenya, Uganda and Zambia the working language of business and design is English; in Tanzania it is Swahili (see Languages by country). In Kenya rendered local lines are Swahili, often blended with Sheng, the urban youth vernacular that mixes Swahili, English and Kenyan slang. Blend naturally the way people actually speak, an English line with a Swahili beat or the reverse, and put the emotional line in Swahili with the functional line in English. Sheng is youth and urban register, do not reach for it on a slide aimed at an older or formal audience. Worked examples: "Twende kazi" (let us get to work) as an emotional Swahili beat rendered as a headline, paired with a smaller English functional line beneath it. "Haina noma" (it is no big deal, it is fine) as a plain confidence line in Sheng register, sized as a pull quote rather than a dense paragraph. Rendered Swahili and Sheng type generally behaves like English in a display face, no unusual diacritics, but keep lines short, image models still degrade on any line over roughly fifteen words regardless of language. Regional note: Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia and Rwanda each set their own language order; see Languages by country below.

## Media and channels

Chat app forwarding is a genuine distribution channel for a designed slide cropped out of a deck, so test legibility at forwarded thumbnail size. Billboards and street level out of home are viewed at speed from vehicles on arterial roads or at a stationary glance in traffic, both worth designing text zones around if a deck slide will be adapted to that format. Matatu (the shared minibus system, usually vividly branded) and boda boda (motorcycle taxi) culture is a genuine source of graphic texture, hand painted lettering, sticker collage, bold vivid colour blocking, worth drawing on for a challenger or youth brand's design language rather than importing wholesale. Radio remains a strong reach medium outside major urban centres.

## Retail and price

Retail spans large modern chain supermarkets down to neighbourhood mini marts, fresh produce markets, small independent kiosks and dukas, and pharmacy or chemist counters, each with a distinct physical texture worth referencing (open air stalls, hand painted duka signage, chemist counter glass). Mobile money is a genuinely dominant payment behaviour at the till, more so than card payment, a till receipt or payment moment rendered in a deck should reflect a phone tap or scan rather than a card swipe. Small pack, sachet and "kadogo" (small unit) pricing is a real mechanic worth reflecting in any slide showing pack architecture or price points.

## Casting and settings

Cast by context, never by stacked category. Write the setting and let it carry the specificity: "a woman with deep brown skin and short natural hair, in a Nairobi apartment kitchen in morning light" rather than a label. Groups of three or four read as a real household or friend group; groups of six or more read as stock casting. Dress code follows occasion and class register: smart casual for urban middle class settings, uniform or work wear for trade and informal sector settings, kanga and kitenge as everyday wear for many women at markets, at home and on the coast as well as in festive settings, cast where the scene calls for them and never used as generic decoration. Outside Nairobi, Mombasa carries coastal Swahili architecture and warmer, hazier light; Kisumu carries lakeside cultural context; Kampala, Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar, Lusaka and Kigali each carry their own geography and street character, do not reuse Nairobi's texture for them without checking.

## Cultural moments pointer

Research the current year's dates before using any of these, several move on the lunar or agricultural calendar. Recurring anchors to check: Jamhuri Day (independence, mid December), Easter, Ramadan and Eid, the back to school periods (January and May), the festive season running from late November through December, and harvest or planting season markers relevant to agricultural categories.

## Regulatory notes

Alcohol and tobacco categories carry advertising restrictions on audience, watershed and imagery that a deck's slide copy and casting must observe. Health, hygiene and efficacy claims (kills a stated percentage of germs, clinically proven) need a cleared substantiation line before they are rendered as on-slide copy, flag any unverified claim rather than rendering it. Sponsorship and tournament work carries flag and jersey restrictions when a major sporting event is involved. Child safety protocols apply whenever a minor appears in casting.

## Languages by country

Set the language order from the country, not from Kenya.

- Kenya: English and Swahili are both official. Business and advertising run in English; the local line is Swahili, with Sheng for young urban audiences in Nairobi.
- Uganda: English leads in business and advertising. Swahili is official but is not the household language in Kampala. Luganda leads in Kampala and the central region, and other regions use their own languages (for example Runyankore in the west, Acholi or Lango in the north, Lusoga in the east). Write the local line in Luganda for Kampala and have a native writer check it.
- Tanzania: Swahili leads in daily life, media, business and advertising. Write Swahili first and English second, if at all. Coastal and Zanzibar Swahili differ from upcountry speech.
- Rwanda: Kinyarwanda leads, with English and French in business.
- Zambia: English leads in business. Bemba, Nyanja and other languages lead by region, not Swahili.
- Never reuse a Kenyan Sheng line unchanged in another country, and when a brief spans two countries write a local line for each.
