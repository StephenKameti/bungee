# Market Module: <Market name>

Copy this file to `references/markets/<market-slug>.md` and fill every section from a real brief, real research, or a client's own guidance. Do not publish a market module built from stereotype or guesswork. Delete this instruction block once filled.

## Audience and society

Who this market's decks are actually speaking to and how they read a room. Cover: what signals trust versus what reads as slick or foreign, whether the audience skews toward formality or informality in board and client settings, class or income register cues worth knowing (without stacking category words), and any social structures, savings groups, trade associations, community networks, worth knowing as a source of texture or targeting.

## Language and copy rules

The working language of business and design in this market, and the local language or register a deck's rendered copy should carry. Cover: how the two blend naturally in real speech and print, which register (formal, youth slang, regional dialect) fits which audience, at least two worked example lines with an English gloss, and how local-language type actually renders and reads in this script (spacing, diacritics, character behaviour in a display face) so image-model text rendering does not break it.

## Media and channels

Which channels actually carry design and distribution in this market: the dominant chat or social forwarding behaviour, how out-of-home is actually viewed, any channel with outsized reach a global default list would miss, and anything about local print, broadcast or outdoor production worth designing decks around.

## Retail and price

The retail formats and price mechanics that give this market its texture: modern trade formats described generically, general or informal trade if it is a meaningful channel, receipts, tags, signage and till culture worth referencing as design devices, and anything distinctive about how people pay that a deck's retail mechanics should get right.

## Casting and settings

How this market's people, places and light should be described by context, not by stacked category words. Cover: everyday register versus stock or editorial register, real neighbourhoods, streets and settings worth naming instead of a generic label, and how light and season change across the year and across regions inside the market.

## Cultural moments pointer

Do not hardcode dates from memory, since public holidays, religious observances and cultural moments shift year to year or are lunar. List the recurring anchors worth checking for the live year and instruct that current year dates always get confirmed before use.

## Regulatory notes

Claims, disclosure and category rules that affect what a deck can render as on-slide copy: health and efficacy claim limits, alcohol or age-restricted category rules, financial services disclosure norms, advertising standards body requirements, and anything else that would need legal sign-off before the rendered copy ships.
