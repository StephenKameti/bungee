# Motion Brief

A motion brief turns a still idea into a film anyone can build: a motion designer, an editor, an image to video model, or a code driven motion tool. It is tool agnostic on purpose. Write one whenever a deliverable moves: a bumper, an animated key visual, a short vertical video post, a kinetic type social, a video end frame, a cutdown, or a hero film.

The brief is the film on paper. If the shot list is right, any competent builder gets a good film. If it is vague, no tool saves it.

---

## 1. Anatomy, in this order

1. **Film line.** One sentence: what happens and what the viewer feels at the end.
2. **Spec.** Length, master ratio, the other ratios (all cut from one timeline), frame rate (30 or 60), tempo. Default tempo is 120 BPM: one beat is 0.5 s, one bar is 2 s. Length in bars = seconds x BPM / 240.
3. **Build route,** with one line on why:
   - *Code drawn motion graphics* for kinetic type, product UI, data, logo moves and pack slams. Frame exact, cheapest to revise.
   - *Image to video* from the approved master still, for one photographic movement (steam rising, light shifting, a slow push).
   - *Live action or edit* of supplied footage.
   - *Hybrid:* generated or shot plates with type and graphics animated on top.
4. **Assets.** The approved master still, logo file, pack shots, fonts, footage, music if supplied. Real assets only. Never redraw a logo, a pack or a product screen.
5. **Shot list on the beat grid** (the core of the brief, section 2).
6. **Sound.** Music direction (supplied track, or original: tempo, mood, key), a sound effect for each key action, voiceover script if any (about 2.5 words per second, approved before timing the picture), mix target: about -14 LUFS integrated, true peak at or below -1.5 dBFS, a clean musical ending.
7. **Text on screen.** Every line in the market's language from the market layer, with an English gloss where the reader needs one. Design for sound off: the film must make sense muted.
8. **Lockup and poster frame.** The last settled frame, and the single frame that should be the thumbnail.

---

## 2. The shot list

One row per change. Time it to the beat, not to a stopwatch.

| bar.beat | time s | what is on screen | what changes | camera | sound | text on screen and hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **bar.beat** 1.1 is the first downbeat. 3.1 is the start of bar 3 (4.0 s at 120 BPM).
- **What changes** names the motion: slams in, morphs into, pushes off, splits into, fills. Not "appears".
- **Hold** is how long the text stays fully settled and readable.

---

## 3. Craft laws (the quality bar)

- **Hook in the first 2 seconds:** the problem in about five words of big type, or the single most striking image from the route.
- **Something new every 2 to 4 seconds.** No dead frame. A slow push on the final hold so the lockup is never frozen.
- **Readability:** a short label needs about 0.8 s settled; a sentence about 0.3 s per word, never under 1.2 s. Pace comes from motion and cuts, never from pulling text early.
- **Type minimums** at 1080 px on the short side: headline 84 px, supporting 44 px. Safe area 80 px from the sides, 100 px top and bottom. Check it at 360 px wide, the size of a phone feed.
- **One timeline, every format:** keep the key content inside the central square, or restack side by side elements vertically for 9:16.
- **Beat locking:** the big moments (reveal, payoff, logo) land on downbeats; small sequential events on consecutive beats. Each sound effect lands on the frame its motion starts.
- **Clear before the next idea:** the old layout leaves, then the new one arrives. A crossfade between two busy layouts is a muddy double exposure.
- **Product truth:** the pack, the logo and any product screen are the real assets, moved, cropped or masked, never redrawn or recoloured.
- **Banned:** a centred title on a gradient, everything fading in, generic particle bursts, bouncy easing on product shots, corner labels and frame borders, dead time.

## 4. Format presets

| Film | Length | Bars at 120 BPM | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video end frame | 3 to 5 s | 2 | Endline and pack only. The film already did the talking |
| Bumper | 6 s | 3 | Bar 1: hook and mechanism in one move. Bar 2: the resolve. Bar 3: lockup |
| Animated key visual | 6 to 10 s | 3 to 5 | One movement only. If it loops, the last frame equals the first |
| Short vertical post | 10 to 15 s | 5 to 7, then a 1 s hold | Hook, two beats of value, call to action |
| Kinetic type social | 15 s | 7, then a 1 s hold | Words land on beats, giant type, one accent colour |
| Hero film | 15, 30 or 60 s | 7, 15 or 30 | Hook, build, product moment, payoff, end frame |

## 5. Worked example: a 6 second bumper

Fictional brand: a coffee shop chain opening in a new city. Route: "Morning Light", steam lifting from one cup on a sill at first light.

**Film line:** the first cup of the day lights up the street. **Spec:** 6 s, 9:16 master, 1:1 and 16:9 from the same timeline, 30 fps, 120 BPM. **Route:** image to video for the steam on the approved still; type, cup lockup and logo code drawn on top.

| bar.beat | time s | what is on screen | what changes | camera | sound | text on screen and hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 0.0 | The approved still, sill and cup, pre dawn blue | Light rakes across the sill from the left | Slow push in | soft riser | |
| 1.3 | 1.0 | Same | Steam lifts from the cup, one movement | Push continues | warm pad enters | **The first cup is yours.** (holds to 3.5) |
| 2.1 | 2.0 | Same, now gold light | Headline settles, local line rises beneath | | low pluck on downbeat | Local line in the market's language (holds to 3.5) |
| 2.4 | 3.5 | Same | Type clears upward | | whoosh | |
| 3.1 | 4.0 | Clean side third of the still | Logo and opening date land | Push ends at 6.0 | thump on downbeat | Logo + **Opens Saturday** (holds to 6.0) |

**Poster frame:** 5.0 s.

---

## 6. Close every motion brief with

- **Build first:** the one film or format to make before the others, and why.
- **Check before final:** the three things most likely to break (usually a line that is too long to read, the logo landing off the beat, and the vertical restack).
