# Worked Examples

Two full prompts at the standard, annotated. These come from a real skincare engagement (brand details generalized). Study the block structure, then write fresh. Never copy the skincare vocabulary onto an unrelated brand.

## Example 1 · Floor hero, device: oversized pack as architecture

**Tool:** the default photoreal image model · **Aspect:** 4:5 · **Attach:** all pack shots, hero pack reference critical
**Why line:** The pack becomes the architecture. Visible from the door, unmistakable from across the store.

> Photorealistic retail display mockup photography, high end in store theatre. Register: premium dermatological skincare with sculptural confidence, the craft level of a luxury cosmetics flagship applied to a pharmacy environment. Palette strictly: [Brand Teal] #12B7C9, [Warm Ivory] #F8F5F0, [Soft Sand] #EDE5DA, [Stone] #D6CDC1, [Charcoal] #222222. Materials: smooth matte lacquered forms, warm ivory panels, light oak, brushed brass, pale stone, soft integrated concealed lighting with no visible LED strips or fittings. No chrome, no acrylic gloss, no gold foil, no florals. Casting where people appear: [market appropriate casting, stated explicitly]. The [BRAND] wordmark appears in white only where specified and nothing else is written anywhere on the unit. Every printed panel and shelf talker is completely blank, ready for artwork in post. No straplines, no small print, no readable text of any kind rendered in the image. Use the attached pack references as the exact source of truth for all products, including the oversized sculptural replica, which must reproduce label design, logo, typography, colours and proportions of the real pack exactly at large scale. Do not redesign, translate, paraphrase or respell any label text. A dramatic free standing floor unit in a modern pharmacy built as a two metre tall sculptural replica of the hero serum bottle, standing on a circular pale stone base, its oversized dropper cap reaching toward the ceiling. The giant bottle is finished in smooth matte lacquer reproducing the real label exactly at scale, and it appears to be filled: the lower two thirds glow softly from within with a warm ivory light, as if holding serum, the glow rising through a subtle teal gradient. Wrapping around the base of the bottle at waist height is a curved light oak shelf ring holding the five real products faced outward in neat rows, with small blank ivory shelf talkers. Real shoppers in the aisle behind turn to look, softly out of focus. Clean bright pharmacy lighting with the bottle as the brightest object in frame. Shot on 35mm from a low angle to give the bottle monumental presence, f4, photoreal retail theatre mockup.

**What to notice:**
- Preamble → pack fidelity → unit → scene, in that order, one paragraph.
- The device has a job beyond size: the bottle "appears to be filled" and glows. A giant dead replica is a prop; a giant replica doing what the product does is theatre.
- Real product stays at reach height on the shelf ring. The sculpture stops traffic, the shelf converts it.
- The low angle instruction is doing real work; eye level flattens monuments.

## Example 2 · Counter top, device: glorifier elevation

**Tool:** the default photoreal image model · **Aspect:** 4:5 · **Attach:** all pack shots, hero pack reference critical
**Why line:** Luxury grammar at pharmacy scale. One product elevated, the range in attendance.

> [Same preamble and pack fidelity blocks as Example 1.] A premium counter top glorifier on a pale stone pharmacy counter, photographed straight on from about eighty centimetres. At its centre, a single hero serum bottle stands elevated on a slim brushed brass column rising from a light oak base, enclosed under a clear glass dome, lit from below through the base so the bottle glows against the counter. Encircling the dome at base level, a shallow warm ivory ring tray holds the four remaining products lying in fitted recesses like instruments in a case, bars naked. A fine teal line is inlaid around the rim of the base and a small [BRAND] wordmark sits in white on the front edge, nothing else written anywhere. The pharmacy behind falls to soft darkness so the glowing dome is the brightest object in frame. Shot on 85mm, f2.8, photoreal retail mockup.

**What to notice:**
- Same family, same uniform, different device. This is how a counter unit stays related to a floor hero without being a shrunk copy of it.
- "Like instruments in a case" is the kind of concrete simile that steers generators better than adjectives like "premium" or "elegant."
- The environment goes dark so the light device wins. Every prompt should end by deciding what the brightest or warmest object in frame is.

## Adapting the standard to other registers

- **Playful FMCG (snacks, kids, soft drinks):** the devices stay, the finish changes. Matte lacquer becomes glossy colour, stone becomes bold laminate, and the frozen motion device (the crumble, the splash, the fizz) usually leads. The one-device law holds hardest here, because playful brands are the quickest to pile on ideas.
- **Luxury (spirits, fragrance, watches):** glorifier and light lead; oversized replicas only if flawlessly finished; add material call outs like smoked glass, travertine, bronze. Human figures rarely help; let the object own the frame.
- **Fresh and food:** narrative structure (field to plate) and texture walls (grain, crumb) lead; light must read warm, never clinical.
- **Conservative registers (medical, baby, financial adjacency):** run the family at lower theatrical volume and keep a fully restrained variant in the appendix; the flag about offering both registers is mandatory here.
