# Copy Craft

The KV is half picture, half line. This is the line half.

---

## 1. Non negotiables

- **No hyphens. No em dashes. Anywhere in client facing copy.** Rewrite the sentence
- Short, punchy, emotive. If a line needs a comma to survive, it needs cutting
- Never write a claim the brief has not cleared. Flag suspended and pending claims inside the card, do not quietly drop them
- Never improve a signed single minded proposition or an approved endline. Use it verbatim
- Read every line out loud before it ships. Copy that cannot be said is copy that will not be repeated

---

## 2. The line stack

Every route carries five lines. Know what each one is for.

| Line | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | Make the picture mean something more than it already does | 2 to 7 words |
| **Subline** | Make the proposition plain for anyone who did not get it | Under 12 words |
| **Endline** | The thing the brand owns and repeats for three years | 2 to 5 words |
| **Proof callout** | The reason to believe, sat next to the pack | A fragment, not a sentence |
| **CTA** | The next physical action | 3 to 6 words, different per channel |

**The relationship rule:** if the headline just says what the picture shows, delete one of them. The best pairs do different jobs and meet in the middle.

## 3. Headline patterns that travel across markets

These patterns are the shapes, not the words. Fill them from the market layer. The example column below gives one Kenyan example per pattern, since that is the fullest worked market module supplied with this skill, alongside a second example from a different market so the pattern itself is visible and not tied to one place. When the market layer names a different market, write fresh examples from that market's own proper nouns, places and code switch, never reuse these verbatim.

| Pattern | Shape | Example (Kenya) | Example (a second market) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The flip | Say the expected thing backwards | Slow food is not made in a hurry | Real tequila is not made in a lab |
| The plain truth | No craft at all, total confidence | Better than the bakery | It just works |
| The instruction | Imperative, warm, not bossy | Let them get muddy | Deja que jueguen |
| The question nobody asks aloud | Names a private thought | Is one sachet ever enough | Is one plate ever enough |
| The count | A number that reframes the category | Four thousand steps a day | Three generations in the making |
| The local proper noun | A place, a route, a habit only locals know | Born on the coast road, since 1979 | From the Roma neighbourhood, since 1979 |
| The code switch | The local language carrying the emotional beat, the market's working language carrying the information | Twende kazi. The rest is handled | Sin miedo a ensuciarse. It has the stains covered |

## 4. Local language and code switching

See `references/markets/<market>.md` for the specific local language, its street register, and worked local lines for the brief's market. This section is the discipline that applies wherever the market layer supplies a local language alongside the working language of the brief.

The fastest way to lose a room is a forced or dictionary version of the local language.

**Do:**
- Blend naturally the way people actually speak. Working language sentence, local language beat. Or the reverse
- Put the local language on the emotional line and the working language on the functional line
- Use words that live in kitchens, transport and markets, not words that live in dictionaries
- Test the line on the exact audience in the spine. A line for an older shopper in a smaller town is not a line for a younger shopper in the capital, even inside the same market

**Do not:**
- Translate a headline written in the brief's working language into the local language and call it an adaptation
- Reach for street slang in a brief targeting an older or more formal household audience unless there is a reason
- Stack more than one slang or code switched term per line
- Use local language words purely as decoration on a line that is otherwise entirely in the working language

**The test:** would someone say this out loud to a friend without irony? If not, rewrite.

## 5. Copy by channel

| Channel | What changes |
|---|---|
| Billboard | Seven words maximum, headline plus endline, nothing else. No CTA, nobody is writing it down |
| Shelf talker | Benefit plus price cue. The headline is often the CTA |
| Gondola end | Headline at eye height, proof callout below, variant names as a list |
| Social feed | Headline in the image, the argument in the caption, CTA in the caption |
| Story | Four words in image. Everything else is a sticker or a swipe |
| OLV end frame | Endline and pack only. The film did the talking |
| Press | Headline, subline, proof, then the legal. This is the only place a full sentence belongs |
| Trade and B2B | Lead with the commercial argument, not the emotional one. Yield, cost per portion, shelf life, consistency |

## 6. Endline craft

An endline is a three year commitment. Test every candidate against these five.

1. Can it be said in one breath
2. Does it still work when the brand name is removed, and does it get better when it is added back
3. Could a competitor say it. If yes, kill it
4. Does it survive translation into the market's local language without becoming a different promise
5. Will a sales rep say it unprompted in a trade meeting

Give three options, ranged. One elegant, one plain, one shareable.

---

## 7. Common failure modes

- **Pun that only works written.** Radio and word of mouth kill it
- **Line that needs the picture to parse.** Fine for the KV, useless everywhere else
- **Claim inflation.** "Number one" and "the best" without substantiation will get pulled by legal or by the client's lawyer, usually after artwork
- **The brand talking about itself.** "We are passionate about quality" is not copy
- **Two ideas in one line.** Split it or cut one
- **Copy written for the client, not the shopper.** If the line contains the word "solution", start again
