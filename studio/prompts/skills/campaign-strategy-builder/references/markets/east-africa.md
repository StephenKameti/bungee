# Market Module: East Africa

This is a worked market module. Load it when the brief states Kenya, or another East African market, as the market. Treat Kenya as the fullest reference inside the region; where another East African market diverges from it, that is noted inline. When the brief names a different market entirely, do not reuse this file, build or load the matching one instead.

---

## Audience and society

Nairobi neighbourhoods carry distinct class and character signals worth building into the audience architecture: Kilimani and Lavington read upper middle class and leafy; Eastlands neighbourhoods like Umoja or Buruburu read working and lower middle class, dense low rise housing, shared minibus culture visible; informal settlements should only appear in a strategy when the brief is explicitly about that context, handled with care and never as generic set dressing. Chamas, informal savings and investment groups, are a genuine social structure worth building into Tier 2 or Tier 3 for any campaign touching household saving, group buying or community trust. Household and shopper audiences are often women led in FMCG categories, but confirm against the brief rather than assume; trade and distributor audiences skew male in most on the ground FMCG contexts. Outside Nairobi, Mombasa carries a coastal, Swahili coast character; Kisumu carries lakeside cultural context; smaller towns and rural counties carry agricultural and market town character, not a downgraded version of Nairobi.

## Language and copy rules

The dominant working language of business and advertising is English. The dominant local language for the local line is Swahili, often blended with Sheng, the urban youth vernacular that mixes Swahili, English and Kenyan slang. Blend naturally the way people actually speak, an English sentence with a Swahili beat or the reverse, put the emotional line in Swahili and the functional line in English. Sheng is youth and urban register, do not reach for it in a brief targeting an older or formal audience without a specific reason. Worked examples: "Twende kazi" (let us get to work) as an emotional Swahili beat paired with an English functional line, the shape used for an energy or action proposition. "Haina noma" (it is no big deal, it is fine) as a plain confidence line in Sheng register, the shape used for a reassurance proposition. Tone rule specific to this market: avoid a line that reads like a translated head office brief, test every local line spoken aloud by someone who actually speaks the register. Regional note: Uganda and Tanzania are also Swahili speaking, but local slang and code switch differ from Kenyan Sheng, do not reuse a Kenyan line unchanged. Zambia's dominant local languages are Bemba, Nyanja and others depending on region, not Swahili. Rwanda's dominant local language is Kinyarwanda, also not Swahili.

## Media and channels

Standard channel set for FMCG in this market: free-to-air television, radio split across English adult contemporary, Swahili mass market, and Sheng youth or vernacular stations, out of home on the major arterial roads out of Nairobi and in the CBD plus neighbourhood-level boards, digital social split across short video, image-led and community platforms by audience tier, messaging-app group seeding and chama network sharing, activation and below the line work at neighbourhood, supermarket, market and school level, and influencer or earned media partnerships. Chat app forwarding is a genuine distribution channel, often outperforming the feed post it was cropped from, always test legibility at forwarded thumbnail size. Billboards and street level out of home are viewed at speed from vehicles on arterial roads, or at a stationary glance in traffic. Matatu and bus branding is a high frequency channel; expect windows and doors to obscure roughly a third of any wraparound design. Radio remains a strong reach medium outside major urban centres. For youth campaigns, add the market's leading short video and video-sharing platforms plus neighbourhood activations and school gate presence.

## Retail and price

Supermarket format ranges from large modern chain supermarkets in urban malls down to mid size neighbourhood supermarkets and mini marts. Open air and informal trade is a major channel, not a fallback: fresh produce markets, kiosks, dukas and roadside vendors carry a large share of shopper volume, especially outside major cities. Pharmacies and chemists are a distinct, smaller format, relevant for health and personal care categories. Mobile money is a genuinely dominant payment behaviour at the till, more so than card payment; a payment moment in creative should show a phone tap or scan rather than a card swipe. Small pack, sachet and "kadogo" (small unit) pricing is a real mechanic worth reflecting in creative territories and executions for price sensitive categories.

## Casting and settings

Cast by context, never by stacked category: write "a woman with deep brown skin and short natural hair, photographed in a Nairobi apartment kitchen in morning light" rather than a label. Groups of three or four read as a real household or friend group; groups of six or more read as stock casting. Dress code follows occasion and class register: smart casual for urban middle class settings, uniform or work wear for trade and informal sector settings, kanga or kitenge patterned fabric only when the brief calls for a traditional or festive register, never as generic decoration. Regional notes: Kampala has its own hill geography and boda boda density; Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar carry a stronger Swahili coast and Islamic cultural register; Lusaka and Kigali are calmer, more planned cityscapes with different traffic and street character. Do not reuse Kenyan street texture for these without checking.

## Cultural moments pointer

Research the current year's dates before using any of these, several move on the lunar or agricultural calendar. Recurring anchors to check: Jamhuri Day (independence, mid December), Easter, Ramadan and Eid, the back to school periods (January and May), the festive season running from late November through December, harvest and planting season markers relevant to agricultural categories, and county or regional cultural events where the brief's specific county is named.

## Regulatory notes

Alcohol advertising carries audience, watershed and imagery restrictions. Health, hygiene and efficacy claims (kills a stated percentage of germs, clinically proven) need a cleared substantiation line before they run, flag any unverified claim for legal sign off rather than shipping it. Sporting tournament work carries national flag and jersey restrictions, and sponsorship ambush marketing rules if the brand is not an official partner. Child safety protocols apply whenever a minor appears in casting. Financial services campaigns carry disclosure requirements on rates, terms and risk that the copy suite's supers and disclaimers must reflect.
